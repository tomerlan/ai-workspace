# Life as a Semiconductor: Jack Kruse’s Evolutionary Theory

Jack Kruse’s central claim is that life did not primarily evolve as a “bag of chemistry,” but as a **solid‑state, light‑responsive semiconductor system** embedded in water. In his framing, evolution is the long process of refining biological materials that can **absorb, transform, store, and emit light**—and using that light as both **energy** and **information** to control metabolism, growth, and development. The semiconductor is not a metaphor for him; it is the *selection pressure* that explains why certain molecules (melanin, aromatic amino acids, DHA, collagen, etc.) became foundational.

What follows reconstructs that narrative in a clear, standalone way, while labeling what is established science versus Kruse’s extrapolation.

---

## 1. What is a semiconductor? (brief setup)

A **semiconductor** is a material that can behave sometimes like an **insulator** (blocking electron flow) and sometimes like a **conductor** (allowing electron flow), depending on conditions like light, temperature, electric fields, or impurities.

Plain-language analogy: think of a semiconductor as a **valve** in an electrical circuit—more controllable than a wire, less blocking than rubber.

Key terms:

- **Valence band / conduction band**: In solids, electrons occupy allowed “energy floors.”  
  - The **valence band** is the highest “filled” floor.  
  - The **conduction band** is a higher floor where electrons can move freely and carry current.

- **Band gap**: The **energy jump** required to lift an electron from the valence band into the conduction band. Light can provide that jump. Band gap size determines what wavelengths a material interacts with.

- **Wide-gap vs narrow-gap semiconductors**:  
  - **Narrow band gap**: smaller jump; responds to lower-energy light (often infrared/visible).  
  - **Wide band gap**: larger jump; can handle higher energies and can interact with **UV** and higher-frequency effects.

- **Doping**: Adding small amounts of other atoms to change conductivity (like adding a pinch of salt to change how water conducts). In electronics, doping creates **n-type** (more electron carriers) or **p-type** (more “holes,” i.e., missing electrons that behave like positive carriers).

- **Topological insulator (TI)**: A material that is an **insulator inside** but has **conductive surfaces/edges** that are unusually robust. (Gloss: “topological” here means the conduction is protected by the material’s global structure, not easily disrupted by small defects.)

Kruse uses these concepts to argue that biology is built from **hydrated, carbon-based semiconductors** whose “circuit behavior” is tuned by light and by the atoms associated with proteins and membranes.

## 2. The evolutionary narrative (Kruse’s core story)

### 2.1 Ancient Earth light environment: why spectrum matters

**Kruse argues** that the *spectrum of light reaching early Earth* was a primary evolutionary constraint. Light is not just energy; it is also **information** (in his language: frequency encodes energy, and light’s structure—he emphasizes orbital angular momentum—encodes information).

Why spectrum matters in his view:

- Molecules that survive and function under a given light environment must be able to:
  - **absorb** certain wavelengths without being destroyed,
  - **convert** light into charge separation (electrons/holes),
  - **emit** light at useful wavelengths for signaling or catalysis,
  - and do this in water.

He repeatedly emphasizes that terrestrial sunlight today lacks the deepest UV (especially **UVC** and **vacuum UV, VUV**) at ground level because the atmosphere absorbs it. Yet he claims biology still uses **ultraweak UV** internally—implying organisms evolved materials that can **up-convert** or generate higher-energy photons locally.

**Mainstream biology holds** that early evolution was shaped by UV stress, redox chemistry, and later oxygenation; it does not generally frame selection as “semiconductor optimization,” though photochemistry and pigment evolution are well-established.

---

### 2.2 Prokaryotes as primitive semiconductors: “5000× more biophotons”

**Kruse argues** (citing work he attributes to Popp/van Wijk traditions) that **prokaryotes emit ~5000× more biophotons than eukaryotes**. Here “biophotons” means **ultraweak photon emission** from living systems (real phenomenon; measured as extremely low-level light output).

How he interprets this:

- **Early life was optically loud** because it relied more directly on light-driven redox chemistry and less on compartmentalized, mitochondria-centered energy management.
- High emission is framed as a sign of:
  - less “refined” internal control,
  - more direct interaction between environmental light and cellular charge carriers,
  - and a need for **optical signaling** in a world without complex multicellular coordination.

**What semiconductor function did this serve (in Kruse’s framing)?**
- Biophoton emission becomes a kind of **internal optical wiring**:
  - photons act like “bits” that coordinate reactions,
  - UV-range photons in particular act as **mitogenic signals** (signals linked to cell division),
  - and emission patterns reflect the cell’s redox state (its electron availability).

**Established vs speculative**
- **Established**: ultraweak photon emission exists and changes with oxidative stress and metabolism.  
- **Speculative (Kruse)**: that this emission is a primary *control layer* akin to engineered photonic circuits, and that the prokaryote/eukaryote difference is best explained as semiconductor “chip redesign.”

---

### 2.3 The prokaryote → eukaryote transition: endosymbiosis as “internalizing the semiconductor”

**Mainstream biology holds** that eukaryotes arose when an archaeal host incorporated bacteria that became **mitochondria** (endosymbiotic theory). This enabled efficient oxidative phosphorylation and complex cellular organization.

**Kruse’s semiconductor lens** reframes this as a major architectural shift:

- Prokaryotes: semiconductor behavior is more **surface-exposed** and environmentally driven.
- Eukaryotes: the key innovation is **internalizing** the energy-and-light transduction machinery into organelles (mitochondria), creating:
  - better control of charge separation,
  - better management of reactive oxygen species (ROS),
  - and reduced “waste” photon emission because energy is more efficiently captured and routed.

In his telling, eukaryotes emit less light because they became **better at trapping and using it**—like improving a circuit so less energy leaks as heat or stray radiation.

He also links this to the idea that cells evolved something like **biological photolithography**: circadian light/dark cycles act as “optical tweezers” that place atoms and tune proteins over time, analogous to how chip fabs use light to pattern silicon.

**Established vs speculative**
- **Established**: mitochondria changed energy efficiency and redox control; circadian regulation is pervasive.  
- **Speculative (Kruse)**: that endosymbiosis is best understood as a semiconductor redesign whose key output is altered biophoton spectra and internal UV/VUV signaling.

---

### 2.4 Melanin and the Cambrian explosion: wide-gap semiconductor as evolutionary accelerator

**Kruse argues** that **melanin** is an ancient, central biological semiconductor and that its emergence/expansion as a **wide band gap** material is tied to the **Cambrian explosion** (~540 million years ago), when animal body plans diversified rapidly.

His argument (as he presents it across posts) has several parts:

- **Melanin is a wide band gap semiconductor**: wide-gap materials can interact with higher-energy photons and tolerate harsh conditions (heat, radiation).
- Melanin’s role is not just pigmentation; it is a **light-energy transducer** that can:
  - absorb broadly across wavelengths,
  - convert light into electronic excitations,
  - and (he claims) help generate internal UV-range signals used for control.

Why melanin specifically?
- It is **broadband** (absorbs across a wide spectrum).
- It is **chemically robust** (stable polymeric pigment).
- It appears in many tissues beyond skin (e.g., eye structures; he emphasizes neuroectoderm-derived melanins).
- He treats it as a foundational “hardware layer” for later complexity, especially in nervous systems.

**Mainstream view**
- Melanin is a pigment with photoprotective roles and complex biochemistry; it also has interesting electronic properties studied in materials science.  
- The claim that melanin drove the Cambrian explosion as a semiconductor upgrade is **not mainstream**; Cambrian explanations typically involve oxygen levels, ecological interactions, developmental gene networks, and environmental change.

### 2.5 Aromatic amino acids: “critical circuit points” selected for optical/electronic behavior

The **aromatic amino acids** are:
- **Tryptophan**
- **Tyrosine**
- **Phenylalanine**

“Aromatic” means they contain ring-shaped electron systems (benzene-like rings) with **delocalized electrons**, which makes them strong **UV absorbers** and useful in electron transfer.

**Kruse argues** evolution selected these amino acids for “circuit nodes” because:

- Their ring electrons make them good for **electron hopping / charge transfer** (moving electrons through proteins).
- They have strong, specific **absorption spectra** in the UV range, so they can act like **optical switches** or sensors.
- They appear disproportionately in key proteins and active sites, which he interprets as evidence of **semiconductor design logic**: put the best opto-electronic components at control bottlenecks.

He also links aromatic amino acids to:
- **dopamine and thyroid hormone synthesis** (tyrosine as precursor),
- and to photochemical transformations (he suggests UV/VUV-driven chemistry creates or modulates signaling molecules).

**Established vs speculative**
- **Established**: aromatic residues participate in electron transfer, UV absorption, and protein photophysics; they are common in active sites.  
- **Speculative (Kruse)**: that their evolutionary selection is primarily because they are semiconductor “components” for optical control via endogenous UV/VUV signaling.

---

### 2.6 DHA: why he treats it as co-evolving with the semiconductor system

**DHA (docosahexaenoic acid)** is a highly unsaturated omega‑3 fatty acid enriched in:
- neuronal membranes,
- and the retina.

**Mainstream biology holds** DHA affects membrane fluidity, signaling, and neural/visual function.

**Kruse argues** DHA is special because:

- Its many double bonds create a **highly polarizable electron cloud**, making it unusually responsive to electromagnetic and photonic effects.
- In the retina and brain, DHA helps build membranes that support:
  - fast signaling,
  - high sensitivity to light,
  - and (in his framing) better semiconductor-like behavior at membrane interfaces.

He links DHA to “semiconductive abilities” of central retinal pathways and treats it as part of the same evolutionary arc as melanin: better light handling → better neural computation.

**Established vs speculative**
- **Established**: DHA is critical for retinal and brain function.  
- **Speculative (Kruse)**: that DHA’s primary evolutionary role is as an opto-electronic semiconductor cofactor enabling topological-insulator-like behavior in tissues.

---

### 2.7 The progression: a Kruse-style timeline toward “human cells as wide-gap topological insulators”

Putting his narrative into a single arc (simplified):

1. **Pre-life / early chemistry in water**
   - Light is abundant; water is the medium.
   - Selection begins around materials that can manage charge and photons.

2. **Prokaryotes**
   - Primitive semiconductor systems; high biophoton emission.
   - Strong coupling to environment; less internal control.

3. **Eukaryotes + mitochondria**
   - Endosymbiosis internalizes energy transduction.
   - Better redox control; less photon “leakage”; more complex regulation.

4. **Rise of wide band gap biological materials**
   - Expansion of hydrated carbon-based semiconductors (he emphasizes collagen and other protein-water systems).
   - Increasing ability to generate/use UV-range signals internally.

5. **Cambrian-era complexity and melanin**
   - Melanin as a wide-gap semiconductor becomes central for managing light and enabling complex body plans.

6. **Neural/retinal specialization**
   - Aromatic amino acids placed at “circuit choke points.”
   - DHA-rich membranes enable high-performance signaling in retina/brain.

7. **End-state (his claim): humans as wide-gap topological insulators**
   - Human tissues behave like **wide band gap semiconductors** with **topological insulator** features: insulating bulk, conductive surfaces/interfaces.
   - The culmination is a system that can:
     - harvest sunlight,
     - transform it into internal signaling light (including UV/VUV in his view),
     - and coordinate organism-wide function through water, collagen, melanin, and mitochondria.

This “end-state” is a philosophical capstone for him: evolution trends toward **higher control of light and charge** with lower energy waste—like progressively better chip design.

## 3. What the biological semiconductor actually is in modern cells (Kruse’s map)

Kruse’s “biological semiconductor” is not one molecule; it’s a **stack** of interacting materials, mostly **hydrated** (water-structured) and **carbon-based**.

He commonly highlights:

- **Melanin**  
  - Role (Kruse): broadband absorber/transducer; source of endogenous UV/VUV signaling; central to neuroendocrine control (POMC/melanocortin axis).  
  - Light interaction: broad absorption; he emphasizes UV-related signaling.

- **Water (structured / “coherent domains”)**  
  - Definition: water near surfaces can have different ordering than bulk water.  
  - Role (Kruse): photonic/charge storage medium; “memory” substrate for light information; enables long-range coherence.

- **Collagen (and other structural proteins)**  
  - Role (Kruse): wide band gap semiconductive scaffold; especially important in connective tissue and bone; acts like a “wafer.”  
  - He cites Becker-style ideas that collagen can show semiconductor-like behavior.

- **Cytoskeleton (microtubules/actin networks)**  
  - Role (Kruse): structural + electrical/optical routing; part of the “circuit board” architecture.

- **DNA**  
  - Role (Kruse): not just genetic code but an electronic/photonic element interacting with biophotons and aromatic bases.

- **Aromatic amino acids in proteins**
  - Role: UV absorbers and electron-transfer nodes; “optical control points.”

- **Mitochondria**
  - Role: central energy transducer; generates ROS/RNS (reactive oxygen/nitrogen species) that also act as signals; produces water; couples redox to electromagnetic effects.

Mechanistically, he treats cells as devices that **transduce** (convert) energy/information across forms:

- photon → electronic excitation (charge separation)
- electronic excitation → chemical work (metabolism)
- electronic excitation → photon emission (biophotons as signals)
- all stabilized and routed by structured water and protein scaffolds

## 4. What breaks — disease through this lens

If organisms are evolved semiconductors, then disease is not merely “bad chemistry,” but **loss of semiconductor order**: disrupted band gaps, altered charge carrier flow, degraded photonic signaling, and broken coupling between light, water, and redox state.

### Cancer (Kruse’s framing)
**Kruse argues** cancer is fundamentally an **optical signaling failure**:

- Cells require endogenous ultraweak UV (he emphasizes UV/VUV) for proper control of mitosis (cell division).
- When tissues lose the ability to generate the right light signals (due to low redox / damaged semiconductors), cells:
  - fail normal mitotic control,
  - become more mobile,
  - and migrate seeking environments with the missing signals—he analogizes this to metastasis as a “search for UV.”

This is far outside mainstream oncology, which explains metastasis via genetic/epigenetic changes, microenvironment selection, immune evasion, and signaling networks—though metabolism and redox state are recognized contributors.

### Neurodegeneration
In this lens, neurodegeneration reflects:

- failure of high-performance semiconductor tissues (retina/brain),
- loss of DHA-rich membrane function,
- melanin system disruption in neuroectoderm-derived structures,
- and mitochondrial redox collapse leading to noisy or insufficient signaling.

Mainstream neurology emphasizes protein aggregation, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, vascular factors, and genetics; Kruse reinterprets these as downstream of broken light-water-redox coupling.

### nnEMF / artificial light as disruptors
Definitions:
- **Artificial light at night (ALAN)**: light exposure during biological night, especially blue-enriched LEDs.
- **nnEMF**: “non-native electromagnetic fields,” typically referring to man-made RF/microwave and other exposures.

**Kruse argues** these disrupt biology like interference in a chip:

- Blue-heavy light without natural spectral balance alters circadian timing and retinal signaling.
- nnEMF adds electromagnetic noise that perturbs charge separation, water structuring, and mitochondrial function.
- The result is degraded semiconductor behavior: worse coherence, worse redox, altered signaling, and disease.

**Established vs speculative**
- **Established**: circadian disruption and nighttime light affect metabolism, sleep, and health; RF bioeffects are debated and depend on exposure parameters.  
- **Speculative (Kruse)**: that these exposures directly “detune band gaps,” collapse topological-insulator-like protection, and specifically abolish endogenous UV/VUV control signals as a primary disease mechanism.

## 5. Where this overlaps with mainstream science vs. where Kruse extrapolates

### Overlaps with established or active mainstream research

- **Quantum biology is real (in limited domains)**  
  Examples widely discussed in the literature include:
  - quantum coherence effects in **photosynthesis** energy transfer,
  - **radical pair** mechanisms in avian magnetoreception (a quantum-sensitive chemical compass),
  - **electron/proton tunneling** in enzymes.

- **Biophoton emission is measured**
  Ultraweak photon emission from cells and tissues is experimentally observed and correlates with oxidative processes and metabolic state.

- **Biomolecules can have semiconductor-like properties**
  Some proteins, pigments, and biopolymers show interesting electronic conduction, photoconductivity, and charge-transfer behavior—especially when hydrated or structured.

- **Light as a biological regulator**
  Photobiology is mainstream: circadian photoreception, retinal signaling, UV effects on skin, nitric oxide release, and photochemical reactions are well established.

### Where Kruse goes beyond what is established (and why it’s controversial)

- **“Life evolved primarily as a semiconductor device”**
  This is a sweeping reinterpretation. Mainstream evolutionary biology does not treat semiconductor optimization as the dominant selection pressure across all life.

- **Endogenous VUV/UVC as a central control signal**
  Kruse repeatedly emphasizes internal generation of very high-energy UV (including VUV). While cells can emit ultraweak photons, the claim that organisms routinely generate and use **VUV** as a primary signaling layer is not established and faces physical constraints (absorption in water and tissues, measurement challenges, unclear mechanisms).

- **Cancer as “cells searching for UV light”**
  This is not a mainstream model of oncogenesis or metastasis. It reframes complex genetic, epigenetic, and ecological tumor dynamics into a single optical-control failure story.

- **Humans as wide-gap topological insulators (biologically)**
  “Topological insulator” is a precise condensed-matter term. Using it for whole cells or tissues is, at minimum, an analogy unless specific, testable TI signatures are demonstrated in vivo under physiological conditions.

- **Circadian cycles as “photolithography”**
  This is evocative and may capture something true about light-driven regulation, but it is not an established mechanistic equivalence to semiconductor fabrication.

## Closing synthesis

Kruse’s evolutionary theory can be summarized as: **biology is a light-driven solid-state system built from hydrated carbon-based semiconductors**, and evolution is the story of improving that system’s ability to **capture sunlight, transform it into usable internal signals (including UV-range signals in his view), and route energy/information through water-structured circuits**. In this framing, molecules like **melanin**, **aromatic amino acids**, **collagen**, and **DHA** are not merely biochemical conveniences—they are the *hardware choices* that made complex life possible.

Where the theory is strongest is in pushing readers to take seriously that:
- life is deeply shaped by **photons**, **redox**, and **electronic structure**, not just “chemical reactions in solution.”

Where it becomes most speculative is in treating:
- semiconductor and topological-insulator physics as the **primary explanatory layer** for evolution and disease, often outrunning what has been directly measured in living tissues.

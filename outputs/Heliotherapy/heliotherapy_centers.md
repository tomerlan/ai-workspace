# Heliotherapy: Historical Context & Current Practitioners

Clinics and physicians whose approach includes sunlight, UV, or the UV → melanin/vitamin D biological axis as part of cancer care. The central distinction maintained throughout: **direct UV as biological signal** (light interacts with the body's own chromophores — melanin, hemoglobin, cytochrome c oxidase, immune cells) vs. **photosensitizer-activated light** (light triggers a synthetic drug pre-loaded into cancer cells — mechanistically closer to targeted oxidative chemotherapy).

No modern clinic practices Rollier-style graduated full-body sun exposure as primary cancer treatment. The closest active forms are UV blood irradiation (UBI/UVBI) clinics, which extend the same biological logic — UV directly modifying blood cells to reorganize immune function — without requiring a drug. PDT/SPDT clinics are indexed here for completeness but are categorized as secondary.

---

## Historical Foundation

Classical heliotherapy — prescribed, graduated full-body sunlight exposure as primary treatment — reached its peak in the Alpine sanatorium tradition, 1900–1940s. It was not replaced by disproof; it was displaced by chemotherapy. Three founders established the clinical record: **Niels Finsen** (Copenhagen, Nobel 1903; concentrated arc-lamp UV for lupus vulgaris), **Oskar Bernhard** (St. Moritz, 1902; first systematic wound and surgical TB sun protocols at 1,800 m altitude), and **Auguste Rollier** (Leysin, 1903–1954; 36 clinics, 1,000+ beds, the most elaborated graduated full-body exposure protocol in the literature).

→ [heliotherapy_history.md](heliotherapy_history.md) — precise protocols for each founder: apparatus specs, session timing, day-by-day progression schedules, contraindications, and primary sources.

## Tier 1: Direct UV as Biological Signal

These clinics use UV or light acting directly on the body's own photoreceptors — melanin, hemoglobin, cytochrome c oxidase, immune cells. The light IS the therapy; no photosensitizer drug is involved. This is the mechanistic tradition of Rollier: UV reorganizes biology by interacting with endogenous chromophores, not by triggering a synthetic molecule pre-loaded into cancer cells.

### Issels Integrative Immuno-Oncology
**Location:** Santa Barbara, CA (active outpatient) + Mexico partner clinic (inpatient) — [issels.com](https://issels.com)

**Core strategy:** UV blood irradiation (UBI) as an explicit core component since Dr. Josef Issels founded the program in Germany in 1951. A measured volume of the patient's blood is withdrawn, passed twice through a quartz glass chamber under controlled UV-A light, then reinfused in a closed circuit (~30 min per session). The UV acts directly on blood cells and any pathogens or tumor antigens in the drawn sample; UV-attenuated cells become highly immunogenic on reinfusion, triggering dendritic cell maturation and broad immune activation including NK cells, IL-2, TNF, and interferon upregulation. Warburg's oxygen-deficiency model of cancer is the underlying frame: UBI restores tissue oxygenation and reverses the fermentative metabolism that sustains cancer cells. Layered with whole-body hyperthermia, dendritic cell therapy, and NK cell therapy.

**Record:** Issels founded the world's first hospital dedicated exclusively to integrative cancer immunotherapy (Germany, 1951), growing to 120 beds by 1970. 90% of patients were those who had already failed conventional treatment. Documented case histories include complete long-term remissions of 28–32 years across breast, lung, liver, bone, brain, and ovarian cancers. Dr. Josef Issels served on the German Federal Government Commission on Cancer (1981–1987). Program is now run by his son Dr. Christian Issels from Santa Barbara. [Case histories](https://issels.com/case-histories/index.html)

**Take:** More historical pedigree than any other clinic on this page. The UBI component has the clearest mechanistic rationale of anything currently practiced. The controversy is real: Wikipedia characterizes the Issels treatment as "unproven," and Josef Issels faced (and survived) fraud and manslaughter charges in 1961 (overturned 1964) — the charges stemmed from treating terminal patients who subsequently died, not from any demonstrated harm. No randomized controlled trial of the full Issels protocol exists. The long-term survivor cases are documented but uncontrolled.

### An Oasis of Healing
**Location:** Mesa, Arizona — [anoasisofhealing.com](https://www.anoasisofhealing.com)

**Core strategy:** UVBI (UV blood irradiation) as a key component of a full integrative cancer protocol. ~5% of the patient's blood is drawn, exposed to UV light, and reinfused. Mechanism framed around oxygenation restoration and immune activation: increased RBC production, upregulated antibody production, anti-inflammatory signaling, and detoxification. Number of UVBI sessions individualized by patient immune status and disease stage. Combined with organic whole-food diet, metabolic therapies, body cleansing, and psychological/spiritual support.

**Leadership:** Dr. Thomas Lodi, MD — 35 years of medical experience; trained in conventional medicine (University of Hawaii, 1985), then spent a decade pursuing integrative cancer training internationally before founding the center in 2006. Licensed in both integrative/homeopathic and allopathic medicine.

**Take:** More explicit about UVBI-for-cancer than most US centers, which tend to position it as immune support rather than a direct anti-tumor agent. Dr. Lodi's background is unusually deep — a conventionally trained physician who made a deliberate mid-career shift to integrative oncology after spending years studying it internationally. The center has a dedicated UVBI page specifically addressing cancer, not just general immune support.

### BioCare Hospital & Wellness
**Location:** Tijuana, Mexico — [biocarehospital.com](https://www.biocarehospital.com/integrative-medicine-at-biocare-hospital)

**Core strategy:** UV blood irradiation (photoluminescence / UBI) as the primary immune-modulating intervention — a small volume of blood is withdrawn, exposed to UV-C or UV-A light, and reinfused; the UV-treated cells act as an autogenous "vaccine" that triggers broad immune activation and NK cell upregulation. Paired with whole-body hyperthermia (40–42°C range) which impairs cancer cells' heat-shock response more than normal cells. IV nutrient therapies (vitamin C, ozone, glutathione) to optimize terrain. Designed explicitly as a complement to, not replacement for, conventional oncology.

**Take:** The quieter, less controversial of the two Tijuana clinics. Founded by Dr. Rodrigo Rodriguez; 4.6/5 average patient satisfaction rating across available reviews. No significant negative press found. The cautious "we complement conventional care, we do not replace it" positioning has kept it out of the regulatory crosshairs that have hit more aggressive Mexican clinics. Less research-forward than Riordan — more clinical services, less published rationale.

### Riordan Clinic
**Location:** Wichita, KS, USA — [riordanclinic.org](https://riordanclinic.org/what-we-do/integrative-oncology-at-riordan-clinic/)

**Core strategy:** Two parallel light-axis components. First, UVBI: blood drawn (35–60 cc), exposed to UV light spanning UVC (germicidal), UVA (immune modulation, anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer properties), and UVB, then reinfused — the most mechanistically complete UV spectrum of any clinic listed, with a written rationale going back to Emmett Knott's 1920s protocol. Riordan's protocol also combines UVBI with ozone as a "first kill" pass before UV exposure. Second, vitamin D3 optimization to 60–100 ng/mL serum — explicitly framed as replicating the evolutionary sunlight baseline (the clinic cites 10,000 IU/day as the calibrated sunlight-equivalent). The backbone anti-tumor therapy is high-dose IV vitamin C (pro-oxidant at pharmacological concentration selectively toxic to catalase-deficient cancer cells), with light-axis therapies framed as the immune and terrain layer.

**Take:** The most credible clinic on this list by conventional markers. Non-profit, US-licensed, board-certified practitioners (FABNO), operating since 1975 with a defined research output. No significant public controversy. The IV vitamin C protocol has the most published supporting data of any therapy offered here. Vitamin D targeting at 60–100 ng/mL reflects genuine engagement with the Garland/Holick literature. Limitation: complementary-only position means patients still need a primary oncologist.

## Tier 2: Photosensitizer-Activated Light

These clinics use light to activate a pre-loaded synthetic photosensitizer that has concentrated in cancer cells. The light triggers a chemical reaction (ROS generation) in a foreign molecule rather than engaging the body's own chromophores. Mechanistically, this is closer to targeted oxidative chemotherapy delivered via light than to the heliotherapy tradition. Interesting and potentially effective, but operating on a different biological logic — the body's own melanin, immune cells, or circadian system are not the active agents.

### Hope4Cancer Treatment Centers
**Locations:** Tijuana (Pacific coast) + Cancun (Caribbean), Mexico — [hope4cancer.com](https://hope4cancer.com/integrative-cancer-treatment/)

**Core strategy:** Sono-Photo Dynamic Therapy (SPDT) as the primary anti-tumor mechanism — a non-toxic photosensitizer (chlorophyll derivative or similar) is administered systemically, preferentially accumulates in metabolically active cancer cells, and is then activated by specific light frequencies to generate reactive oxygen species that destroy the cell from within. Layered with UBI (whole blood treated ex vivo with UV, no drug — the Tier 1 mechanism) as an immune-activation component, near-infrared sauna for hyperthermia, and LED light therapy. Non-cytotoxic framing throughout; no conventional chemo or radiation.

**Take:** The most polarizing name on this list. Skeptic sites (RationalWiki, Quackometer) label it outright quackery; a documented case involving a critically ill British child after SPDT received significant press coverage. Complaints Board shows unresolved patient complaints including a $60K+ spend with rising tumor markers. On the other side, the clinic claims a 76% 2-year survival rate from a chart review of 365 stage-4 patients, and 25 years of continuous operation. No peer-reviewed comparative outcome data. The SPDT mechanism is conceptually distinct from heliotherapy; the UBI component is the more relevant element for this research thread.

### Port Moody Health — Integrative Cancer Centre
**Location:** Port Moody, British Columbia, Canada — [portmoodyhealth.com](https://www.portmoodyhealth.com)

**Core strategy:** Clinical photodynamic therapy (PDT) — a photosensitizer administered IV concentrates preferentially in cancer cells over 24–48 hours; laser light at a specific wavelength is delivered via fiber-optic cable to activate the sensitizer, generating ROS that kill cancer cells through direct apoptosis/necrosis, anti-angiogenesis, and secondary immune activation. Side effect: 4–6 weeks of photosensitivity requiring sun avoidance. Combined with local-regional hyperthermia and IV vitamin C.

**Leadership:** Dr. Sharon Gurm, ND, FABNO. Additional clinical training through BC Cancer Agency.

**Take:** PDT is FDA-cleared for esophageal and lung cancer and practiced at MSK and Johns Hopkins — this is not fringe science. Port Moody distinguishes itself by applying it to a broader tumor range. Being in Canada and naturopathic-led means it operates outside the US regulatory framework. No published outcome data for this specific center. Note the inversion: PDT patients must avoid sunlight during treatment — the photosensitizer makes any UV exposure dangerous. This is the opposite of the heliotherapy logic.

### Cancer Center for Healing
**Location:** Irvine, California — [cancercenterforhealing.com](https://cancercenterforhealing.com)

**Core strategy:** Red and near-infrared light combined with photosensitizers as a primary anti-tumor protocol. Photosensitizer administered IV concentrates in cancer cells; red/infrared light (630–850nm range) activates it to generate ROS — a variation of PDT using non-laser broadband light rather than a focused laser. Additionally: photobiomodulation (red light bed) for mitochondrial stimulation in normal cells (supporting immune function and reducing treatment side effects); FireFly photon therapy for identifying cellular stress and stimulating mitochondrial repair. The framing is explicitly mitochondrial — restoring oxidative phosphorylation capacity in cancer-adjacent normal tissue while disrupting it in cancer cells via oxidative stress.

**Leadership:** Dr. Leigh Erin Connealy, MD — one of the more publicly prominent integrative oncologists in the US.

**Take:** The photosensitizer component is drug-triggered (Tier 2). The photobiomodulation red-light component is direct light-cell interaction without drugs (Tier 1 mechanism) but is framed as support, not primary treatment. The FireFly therapy and some broader claims are less well-characterized. No peer-reviewed outcome publications found for the specific light protocols used here.

## Key Researchers & Practitioners

Physicians and researchers who explicitly work on the sunlight → melanin/vitamin D → cancer axis.

### Dr. Damien Downing, MD
**The Hale Clinic, Harley Street, London** — [drdamiendowning.com](https://www.drdamiendowning.com)

**Core strategy:** Ecological medicine — treat the patient's whole-body environment before targeting any specific pathology. For cancer patients, the primary interventions are sunlight optimization (the original thesis of his 1988 book *Daylight Robbery*) and high-dose nutritional support (vitamin D3, IV vitamin C, zinc, B vitamins, CoQ10). His clinical model frames vitamin D deficiency and sun deprivation not as minor risk factors but as upstream causes of immune dysregulation that enable cancer progression. He works collaboratively with NHS oncology teams rather than replacing conventional care. Also practices photobiomodulation for inflammation, infections, and autoimmune conditions.

President of the British Society for Ecological Medicine. Editor of the *Journal of Nutritional and Environmental Medicine* for 20 years. His 1988 book *Daylight Robbery* was the earliest English-language clinical argument that sun avoidance causes more harm than it prevents — predating Holick's mainstream vitamin D research by over a decade.

**Take:** One of the few physicians who has been clinically advocating for sunlight-as-medicine since the late 1980s. His framing is not UV as a cancer treatment per se, but sunlight deprivation as an upstream cause — and correcting it as a foundational oncology intervention. Lower international profile than Holick or Kruse, but with a longer consistent record and less controversy. Practices in London (Hale Clinic, Harley Street), making him the most accessible European entry point for patients in this framework.

### Dr. Michael F. Holick, MD PhD
**Boston University School of Medicine** — [drmichaelholick.org](https://drmichaelholick.org)

**Core strategy:** Sensible sun exposure — calibrated by skin type (Fitzpatrick I–VI), latitude, season, and time of day — to maintain serum 25(OH)D at 40–60 ng/mL. The intervention is behavioral: replace sun avoidance with sun optimization. Primary cancer application is prevention; Holick publishes dose tables that specify exactly how many minutes at given latitudes and skin types produce 1,000 IU equivalent of vitamin D. His clinical position: the systemic benefits of adequate vitamin D (immune regulation, cell differentiation, anti-proliferative signaling) outweigh the incremental skin cancer risk from sensible exposure.

World's foremost authority on sunlight and vitamin D. Argues for sensible sun exposure — not avoidance — as the appropriate cancer prevention strategy. Publishes clinical sunlight dosing by skin type, season, and latitude. Directly opposes the dermatology consensus on sun avoidance.

**Take:** Genuinely controversial in ways that matter. The dermatology establishment pressured Boston University to remove him from its dermatology department (he remained in endocrinology). A KFF Health News investigation found he had received hundreds of thousands of dollars from the tanning industry, supplement companies, and Quest Diagnostics while publicly advocating for sun exposure and vitamin D testing. The conflict of interest is real and documented. That said, critics concede his underlying science is sound — the dispute is about public health messaging thresholds, not the biology. The key question his critics cannot cleanly answer is whether widespread vitamin D deficiency is less harmful than whatever incremental skin cancer risk comes from sensible sun exposure. He is the most cited figure in this space and his clinical dosing guidance remains the most practically useful.

### Dr. Cedric F. Garland, DrPH
**UC San Diego School of Medicine** — [Garland et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19523595/)

**Core strategy:** Population-level vitamin D optimization — maintain serum 25(OH)D ≥40 ng/mL to interrupt cancer initiation and progression at the cellular adhesion stage. His DINOMIT model identifies the mechanism: vitamin D maintains E-cadherin-mediated cell-cell adhesion; deficiency → **D**isjunction of cells → **I**nitiation of nuclear division → **N**atural selection of anaplastic cells → **O**vergrowth → **M**etastasis → **I**nvolution/Transition. The strategy is not treatment but prevention: achieve population-wide sufficiency and the incidence numbers shift substantially. Garland's work is entirely epidemiological — he does not operate a clinic.

Epidemiologist. Developed the DINOMIT model of vitamin D cancer prevention. Estimated 58,000 fewer US breast cancer cases/yr at 25(OH)D ≥40 ng/mL. ~3,000 published studies on vitamin D–cancer inverse associations.

**Take:** The least controversial name here. Published in peer-reviewed journals, holds academic positions, no significant financial conflict record. The IARC released a skeptical 2008 review that "discounted or omitted important evidence" according to Garland's supporters — mainstream oncology has been slow to act on the vitamin D–cancer association despite the epidemiological volume. Notably, his brother Frank (who co-developed the vitamin D–cancer hypothesis) died of esophageal cancer. Garland's work is observational-epidemiological — strong on association, not yet on causation from RCTs.

### Dr. Jack Kruse, MD
**Kruse Longevity Center** — [jackkruse.com/cancer](https://jackkruse.com/category/cancer)

**Core strategy:** Restore the native light environment. Three-part intervention: (1) **morning UV-A + UV-B + IR-A exposure** at sunrise to entrain circadian biology, charge melanin as a photoelectric energy transducer, and suppress melatonin appropriately; (2) **eliminate artificial blue light** after sunset (screens, LEDs, fluorescent) which decouples the circadian signal and impairs melatonin production and mitochondrial repair; (3) **grounding / magnetism** to restore the DC electric field the body uses alongside light. Cancer, in his framework, is mitochondrial dysfunction driven by chronic light deficiency and mismatch — cells revert to glycolysis (Warburg effect) because the photonic input required for oxidative phosphorylation is absent. The therapeutic strategy therefore does not target the tumor; it restores the conditions under which the body's normal surveillance eliminates it.

Neurosurgeon with a quantum biology framework linking melanin, UV light, mitochondrial function, and cancer. Argues the UV/IR combination in natural sunlight is the reference input the biological system was calibrated for — not a risk to be minimized.

**Take:** The most divisive figure on this list. Followers are intensely loyal; critics (including at least one dedicated skeptic site) describe a community with cult-like dynamics — echo chambers, legal threats against former allies, and social media bans of dissenting voices. The core charge is that he extrapolates legitimate bioelectric and quantum biology science far beyond what the evidence supports, and that his framing makes it difficult to engage critically without being positioned as an enemy. What is not in dispute: his identification of light as a primary biological variable is directionally correct and largely ignored by mainstream medicine. What is disputed: whether his specific quantum electrodynamic framing of melanin, mitochondria, and cancer is mechanistically accurate or unfalsifiable speculation. Useful as a conceptual entry point; should be read critically.

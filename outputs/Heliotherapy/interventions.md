# The Solar Callus Protocol

---

## The Solar Callus

### Rationale

The solar callus is the skin's adaptive state built by gradual, progressive sun exposure without burning. The analogy is fitness training: you don't start someone sedentary with a marathon. The skin that has been progressively conditioned handles UV doses that damage naive skin. The adaptation is real and measurable: more protective melanin, thicker epidermis, better DNA repair capacity, calibrated immune tone.

The cancer connection operates on two levels. **Preventative:** it is the intermittent burn pattern that raises melanoma risk, while chronic regular outdoor exposure is inversely associated with it. People who never build a callus — indoor workers who burn on holiday — are the high-risk group. People with lifelong chronic outdoor exposure are not. Building the callus is therefore a primary prevention strategy. **Curative/supportive:** cancer patients are almost always in the worst possible solar callus state — chronically indoors, vitamin D depleted, light-naive skin. The standard clinical response (avoid UV, supplement D) addresses none of the co-pathways this protocol targets. Rebuilding the callus restores the photobiological baseline and is a targeted biological intervention, not supportive care.

The protocol: build the callus deliberately and safely, using UVI as the dose guide, skin type as the starting point, and the absence of erythema as the progression signal.

### What Is Being Measured and What We Are Optimizing

| Endpoint | What it captures | Target | Evidence basis | Method | Frequency | Cost |
|----------|-----------------|--------|---------------|--------|-----------|------|
| MED (minimum erythema dose — the UV dose that just produces redness at 24h) | Functional adaptation state of skin | **≥ 2× baseline** | Repeated suberythemal UV achieves 1.4–2.3× MED increase [Farr et al. 1999; Felton et al. 2020] | Solar simulator + chromameter; 24h readout | Protocol gates only — not daily; test consumes UV dose and requires fresh skin site | ~€2,000–5,000 device one-time |
| Eu:pheo ratio | Melanin quality — eumelanin protective, pheomelanin generates ROS under UV | **Eumelanin dominant; direction: increase eu, decrease pheo** | Direction established [Del Bino et al. PMC4333058]; threshold undefined | HPLC-AHPO on plucked hair; or in-vivo Raman | Monthly or at key timepoints | ~€100–300/sample |
| Melanin index | Total epidermal melanin volume | **Increase from baseline; no specific target** | Direction only; no calibrated cancer-relevant threshold | Mexameter MX 18 (reflectance 660/880 nm) | Daily feasible — handheld, non-invasive, seconds. Melanin changes slowly; treat as trend data | ~€2,000–4,000 device; zero per reading |
| SC thickness | Epidermal hyperplasia — optical path before UV reaches basal cells | **Increase from baseline** | Direction established [Felton et al. 2020] | 20 MHz ultrasound or OCT | Monthly | Lab/clinic cost |
| Vitamin D (25-OH-D) | Systemic photoproduction — confirms UV reaching skin | **> 40 ng/mL** | Functional sufficiency threshold; not callus-specific | Serum; standard lab | Monthly (half-life ~2–3 weeks; weekly testing adds no information) | ~€20–50/test |
| 8-OHdG + 8-isoprostane (urine, 2–4h post-UV) | General post-UV oxidative load | **Direction: decrease per session as adaptation improves** | *Non-specific* — cannot distinguish UV-driven damage from background. Coarse adaptation proxy, not a mechanism readout. | Urine collected 2–4 h post-exposure | Per session during Phase 1 monitoring | Lab cost per sample |

**Primary benchmark:** MED ≥ 2× baseline. Functional, non-invasive, and grounded in the published adaptive range. Eu:pheo ratio is the mechanistic quality marker — track direction, not a number.

### Intervention Definition

**What:** Graduated full-body UV exposure from natural sunlight, dosed by UVI, below the erythema threshold at all times.

**UVI note:** UVI varies by latitude, season, time of day, and cloud cover. Check real-time UVI (weather app or UV meter) — do not assume from time of day. Winter noon in Oslo may be UVI 1; summer morning in Tel Aviv may already be UVI 6.

**Who is excluded:** Active photosensitizing medications (doxycycline, amiodarone, fluoroquinolones, NSAIDs, certain chemotherapy agents). Active SLE or photodermatosis. Chemotherapy patients: coordinate with oncologist first.

### Protocol

#### Phase 0 — Baseline Assessment

**Fitzpatrick skin type** — ask: *"How does your skin respond to 30–45 minutes of unprotected midday summer sun after a winter indoors?"*

| Type | Appearance | UV response |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| I | Very fair, often freckles | Always burns, never tans |
| II | Fair, light eyes | Usually burns, tans minimally |
| III | Medium | Sometimes burns, tans uniformly |
| IV | Olive/light brown | Rarely burns, tans easily |
| V | Brown | Very rarely burns, tans darkly |
| VI | Dark brown/black | Almost never burns |

Self-report is sufficient. Mexameter at baseline provides a corroborating objective reading.

| IF | THEN |
|----|------|
| Starting protocol | Determine Fitzpatrick type; measure baseline MED and melanin index |
| Skin type I | Flag: higher pheomelanin burden; proceed at minimum doses; monitor eu:pheo ratio |
| Active photosensitizer medications | Do not start |

#### Phase 1 — Foundation (UVI ≤ 3)

**Starting dose by skin type:**

| Skin type | Starting session duration | Body area |
|-----------|--------------------------|-----------|
| I | 5–8 min | Arms + face only |
| II | 10 min | Arms + face |
| III | 15 min | Arms + torso |
| IV | 20 min | Arms + torso |
| V–VI | 25–30 min | Full body |

| IF | THEN |
|----|------|
| Session complete; no redness within 24 h | Increase duration by 3–5 min after 3 consecutive sessions |
| Redness (erythema) within 24 h | Reduce next session by 50%; hold for 7 days; do not increase |
| Skin turns pink during session | End immediately; counts as erythema event |
| New body site introduced | Reset to skin-type minimum for that site; treat as a separate track |
| >3 consecutive days missed | Return to 80% of last successful dose |
| Melanin index not increased after 4 weeks | Check eu:pheo ratio; if pheomelanin-dominant, consider MC1R agonist adjunct |

#### Phase 2 — Higher UVI (gate: MED ≥ 1.4× baseline, no erythema in prior 2 weeks)

| IF | THEN |
|----|------|
| Gate not met | Remain in Phase 1 |
| Gate met | Introduce sessions at UVI 4–6, starting at 50% of current Phase 1 duration |
| No erythema at UVI 4–6 | Increase by 5 min per session every 3 sessions |
| Erythema at UVI 4–6 | Return to UVI ≤ 3; remeasure MED; wait 2 weeks before retry |
| UVI > 8 | Cap at 10 min (type I–II), 20 min (III–IV), 30 min (V–VI) |
| Haze, altitude, snow/water reflection | Default to shorter end of range; these conditions increase effective dose |

#### Nutritional Rules

| IF | THEN |
|----|------|
| High dietary omega-6 (seed oils dominant) | Reduce omega-6 before advancing past Phase 1; linoleic acid amplifies UV-induced inflammation |
| Planning UV session | Omega-3 meal the day before; avoid NSAIDs 12 h prior |
| Taking vitamin C supplement | Pause 48 h before sessions during active melanin-building; high-dose ascorbate inhibits melanogenesis |
| Vitamin D < 30 ng/mL | Continue protocol; do not substitute oral D3 for sunlight |

#### Cancer Patient Modifications

| IF | THEN |
|----|------|
| Active chemotherapy | Consult oncologist; if cleared, start at ½ of skin-type starting dose |
| Immunosuppressed | Start at type I doses regardless of actual skin type; monitor 8-OHdG closely |
| MC1R agonist considered | Pharmacologically accelerates eu:pheo shift and NER upregulation without equivalent UV dose; relevant for patients who cannot tolerate meaningful UV |

### Evidence Summary

| Endpoint | Evidence quality |
|----------|-----------------|
| Repeated suberythemal UV increases MED 1.4–2.3× | High — Farr et al. 1999; Felton et al. 2020 |
| UVB-induced tanning protects against DNA photoproduct formation | High — Del Bino et al. PMC4333058 |
| MC1R/cAMP signaling upregulates NER independently of pigmentation | High — Wolf Horrell et al. Exp Dermatol 2017 |
| Intermittent/burn exposure elevates melanoma risk; chronic occupational exposure inversely associated | Moderate (association) — Gandini et al. 2005 |
| Solar elastosis associates with better melanoma survival | Moderate (association) — Berwick et al. JNCI 2005 |

No prospective trial exists showing a solar callus protocol reduces melanoma incidence. The evidentiary case rests on mechanistic completeness + epidemiological convergence.

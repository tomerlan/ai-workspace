# Heliotherapy: Historical Protocols

Classical heliotherapy — prescribed, graduated full-body sunlight exposure as primary treatment — reached its peak in the Alpine sanatorium tradition, 1900–1940s. It was not replaced by disproof; it was displaced by chemotherapy. The clinical record was substantial.

The historical TB record is directly relevant here. Reports from the Finsen and Rollier eras describe improvement and cure not only in skin TB (lupus vulgaris), but also in extra-cutaneous disease patterns such as bone and pulmonary TB treated in sanatoria. By current standards these were mostly uncontrolled and heterogeneous datasets, but they still establish an important translational fact: light-based protocols can alter non-skin disease trajectories through systemic biology, not only local skin effects. A modern review covering this history is available in the Metzger heliotherapy lecture ([PMC4530709](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4530709/)). The detailed architecture and protocol record is documented in Hobday (1997), drawing directly on Rollier's and Bernhard's original texts: [Sunlight Therapy and Solar Architecture](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025727300063043), *Medical History*, 42: 455–472.

## Finsen: Protocol Detail

**Apparatus:** A custom-built 20,000 candle-power carbon-arc lamp (20× an ordinary street lamp), emitting concentrated violet and UV rays. Light was delivered through four converging telescopic tubes suspended at 45° angles, fitted with rock crystal lenses. Water reservoirs between lenses filtered heat while transmitting the therapeutic UV/violet band. The resulting beam was concentrated approximately 15-fold onto a ~2 cm² treatment area.

**Session structure:** 2 hours daily, to the same spot, repeated across several weeks until healing. Because blood in the dermis absorbs UV before it reaches the pathogen, Finsen applied direct pressure to the treatment site to blanch the skin — forcing blood out of the illuminated field. Without this step, penetration was inadequate. Each session required a trained operator and a dedicated station; the Finsen Institute ran many stations simultaneously to handle patient volume.

**Mechanism:** Finsen believed bactericidal short-wave UV was the active agent. Current understanding ([PMC3014565](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3014565/)) suggests the actual mechanism was singlet oxygen generation from porphyrins in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* when exposed to ~400 nm light (violet/UV-A boundary) — not the far-UV germicidal band. His pressure technique mattered: blood porphyrins would otherwise compete and absorb the dose before it reached the bacilli.

**Scope:** Local, not systemic. Finsen treated skin-surface TB (lupus vulgaris). His investigations into sunbathing for bone/joint and pulmonary TB were less developed; it was Bernhard who first systematized the systemic application.

**Outcomes:** The Copenhagen Institute treated approximately 800 lupus vulgaris patients. Roughly half were fully cured; nearly all of the remainder showed marked improvement. Within a few years of publication, 40 Finsen Institutes had been established across Europe and the United States. The treatment worked — consistently enough to win a Nobel and be replicated at industrial scale across a continent. Failures were mostly cases with very advanced, deeply infiltrating disease where penetration of the 2 cm² beam was insufficient to reach all affected tissue.

## Bernhard: Protocol Detail

**Setting:** St. Moritz, Upper Engadine, Switzerland, ~1,800 m altitude. Thin atmosphere substantially raises UV-B and UV-A flux relative to sea level. Wounds treated either on open verandas or in wards with beds moved to open windows.

**Wound and local TB exposure:**
- Dressings removed; diseased part (wound or tuberculous focus) exposed to direct sun daily.
- Initial duration short; increased by **10–20 minutes per day**, up to a maximum of **3–6 hours** depending on the patient's individual tolerance.
- Full solar spectrum used — no concentrating lenses, no heat filtration. Bernhard explicitly preferred unmodified sunlight over filtered or concentrated light.
- After each session: wound left open to the air. Overnight: covered with aseptic gauze bandage. For large wounds, wire cradles (or wire cages held by plaster-of-Paris bands) were used to prevent friction from bedclothes against healing tissue.
- Contraindication: fresh, uninfected surgical wounds were NOT treated with sun — these received occlusive dressings. Sun was reserved for discharging, infected, or tuberculous wounds.

**Progression to full-body exposure:** Bernhard began with local application (diseased part only). Once good local pigmentation was established, he proceeded to irradiate the whole body — effectively adopting Rollier's graduated systemic approach as a second phase.

**Outcomes:** 858 cures and 120 improvements in first 1,000 surgical TB patients — a ~98% response rate in a population for whom the alternative was radical surgery with frequent recurrence. Wounds that had resisted all conventional treatment dried, granulated normally, and closed under sun exposure. Bernhard specifically noted a rapid analgesic effect — pain reduction preceding visible healing — which was difficult to explain by a purely bactericidal model.

**Source:** Bernhard O (1926). *Light Treatment in Surgery*. Edward Arnold: London. (English translation of 2nd German ed.) — reviewed in [Hobday 1997](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025727300063043), pp. 457–459.

## Rollier: Protocol Detail

**Pre-exposure acclimatization:** On arrival at Leysin patients underwent full medical examination, then **1–2 weeks of open-air treatment without sun exposure** — cold Alpine air to raise metabolic activity before any UV contact. Air temperature was a critical variable: Rollier considered exposure in temperatures above 18°C to be a "hot-air bath, not a sun-bath." Early morning was mandatory — in summer, treatment ran from **6 a.m. to 9 a.m.** (earlier at lower altitudes). This is the opposite of midday beach sun: low sun angle, cool air, lower heat component.

**Patient setup:** Loincloth plus white sheet from head to toe. Head covered with a hat or cloth screen throughout. Patients wheeled onto south-facing balconies or solaria in their beds.

**Graduated exposure schedule** (from *Heliotherapy*, 1st ed., 1923):

| Day | Area exposed | Duration per session | Sessions/day |
|-----|-------------|---------------------|--------------|
| 1 | Feet only | 5 min | 3 (with 10–15 min rest intervals) |
| 2 | Feet + lower legs | Feet: 10 min; lower legs: 5 min | 3 |
| 5 | Feet → lower legs → thighs → abdomen → chest | Feet: 25 min; each proximal segment 5 min less than the one below | 3 |
| ~15 | Full body from start of session | 2–3 hrs total daily (summer); 3–4 hrs (winter) | — |

The table above is a minimum schema. Rollier emphasized it did not override clinical judgment: patient age, disease severity, individual light tolerance, altitude of sun, air temperature, air movement, and atmospheric clarity all required adjustment. Rate-limiting variable: **pigmentation speed**. After ~10 days Rollier assessed tanning rate. Patients who reddened rather than browned (erythema-prone) proceeded much more slowly. Erythema = overexposure = failure: any sunburn indicated the dose had exceeded biological tolerance and the process had to be paused and restarted cautiously.

**Upper thorax rule:** For pulmonary TB or any patient with upper-body disease, exposure of the chest was treated with extreme caution — Rollier wrote that sunlight near the thorax "should be dispensed, so to speak, drop by drop." Premature chest exposure could cause hemoptysis or haemorrhage in compromised lungs. The feet-first sequence originated partly from this safety principle: blood is drawn peripherally, away from viscera.

**Orthopaedics integration:** Rollier abandoned plaster-of-Paris casts as incompatible with heliotherapy — "a plaster-of-Paris dressing robs of sunlight precisely those parts of the body which require it most." He developed custom extension and fixation frameworks that permitted full sun and air access to injured limbs.

**Combined regime:** Graduated sun exposure was paired with cold Alpine air (metabolic activation), specific diet, rest, and Rollier's "Work Cure" — occupational therapy he introduced to prevent institutional deterioration and rebuild vocational capability. He regarded morale as a genuine biological variable in recovery.

**Outcomes:** After WWI: 1,746 of 2,167 patients (~80%) recovered. Among 1,129 surgical TB cases with reported breakdown: 87% cure rate for closed cases, 76% for open cases. Hip TB specifically: 125 of 158 cured; 102 with complete recovery of articular function. Failures were predominantly late-stage presentations — "only among those who had allowed their tuberculosis to enter its most advanced stages." Rollier also reported success treating non-TB conditions including rickets, osteomyelitis, burns, varicose ulcers, septic abscesses, and fractures. These latter results — diverse conditions unrelated to *M. tuberculosis* responding to the same sun exposure protocol — are the most difficult to explain under a bactericidal model and point toward systemic biology as the primary mechanism.

**Source:** Rollier A (1923). *Heliotherapy*. Frowde and Hodder & Stoughton: London; 2nd ed. (1927), Oxford University Press. Full protocol diagrams and text in [Hobday 1997](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025727300063043), pp. 460–462. Original book available at [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/heliotherapy0000arol).

## Mechanism: Bactericidal Hypothesis vs. Systemic Biology

All three practitioners operated under the assumption that UV or sunlight acted on disease by killing the pathogen directly — a bactericidal hypothesis that was coherent with Koch's 1890 demonstration that sunlight was lethal to *M. tuberculosis* in vitro. Finsen's apparatus was explicitly engineered around this: concentrate the germicidal ray, deliver it to the lesion, kill the bacillus. This framing won the Nobel.

The problem is that bactericidal UV does not explain most of what was observed.

**What the bactericidal model cannot account for:**

- **Bernhard curing bone and joint TB with whole-body sun exposure.** Subcutaneous bone and joint tissue receives negligible direct UV. The far-UV germicidal band (UVC) penetrates less than 1 mm of skin and reaches no internal organ. If the mechanism were bactericidal UV, exposure of the feet and legs should have no effect on a hip joint or a spinal vertebra.
- **Rollier's 80% recovery rate in pulmonary TB** — where the sun never touches the lung. His protocol specifically required feet-first, cool-air, early-morning exposure precisely because excessive thoracic sun was dangerous, not therapeutic. The lung improved without the lung being irradiated.
- **Rapid analgesic effects** (Bernhard's observation) preceding any detectable tissue healing. Direct bacterial killing does not produce immediate pain relief.
- **Non-infectious conditions responding** — rickets, fractures, osteomyelitis, varicose ulcers. These have no bacterial pathogen to kill.
- **The tanning requirement.** Rollier's entire protocol was built around inducing progressive whole-body pigmentation. Bactericidal UV does not require tanning; it requires the pathogen to be illuminated. The emphasis on tanning as the clinical indicator of adequate dosing implies the skin itself — its chromophores, its photoproducts — is the active site of the therapeutic response.

**What the systemic biology model explains:**

The modern mechanistic account divides into at least three separable pathways, all activated by UV on skin:

1. **Vitamin D → innate immune activation.** UV-B on skin generates vitamin D3 → 1,25(OH)₂D₃ activates macrophages via toll-like receptor signaling → upregulates the vitamin D receptor → induces cathelicidin, a direct antimicrobial peptide that kills *M. tuberculosis* inside macrophages. This pathway operates systemically — the vitamin D travels in circulation and activates macrophages wherever they encounter pathogen, including bone, joint, and lung. A 2006 study ([PMC2063771](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2063771/)) demonstrated that vitamin D supplementation alongside antibiotics accelerates TB recovery specifically through this macrophage-cathelicidin axis. Rollier's patients, spending months in daily sun at 1,800 m altitude, were almost certainly achieving supraphysiologic vitamin D levels by modern reference ranges.

2. **Nitric oxide release.** UV on skin triggers release of stored nitric oxide from dermal stores → systemic vasodilation, improved tissue oxygenation, and direct innate immune signaling. This pathway is dose-dependent and fast-acting — compatible with Bernhard's observed analgesic effect preceding visible healing.

3. **Immune reorganization via UV-modified circulating cells.** UV interacts directly with blood cells in dermal capillaries. Rollier's patients spent hours daily in sun; this is not negligible UV exposure to the vascular bed. UV-modified lymphocytes and dendritic cells re-entering circulation carry an altered immunological signal — the same mechanism exploited by modern UBI therapy, where blood is deliberately extracted and UV-irradiated ex vivo before reinfusion.

**The implication for cancer:**

The bactericidal framing was always too narrow. It captured the phenomenon Finsen observed (local skin lesions responding to local UV) and was a reasonable hypothesis for 1896. But the clinical record — especially Rollier's systemic results across non-skin TB and non-TB conditions — is consistent with a different and more general mechanism: UV on skin activates a cascade of systemic photobiological signals that reorganize immune competence, metabolic state, and tissue repair capacity throughout the body.

This is exactly the mechanistic argument for heliotherapy in cancer. Not "UV kills cancer cells" (which would be the bactericidal analog — and is not supported). But: UV restores the systemic biological conditions under which normal immune surveillance operates, and which cancer cells and chronic infections alike depend on being absent in order to persist. The target is the host, not the tumor — just as Rollier's target was the patient's biology, not the bacterium directly.

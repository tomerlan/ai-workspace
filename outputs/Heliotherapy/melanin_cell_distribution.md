# Where Is Melanin Found in the Human Body?

---

## Bottom Line

Melanin is found in far more cell types than textbooks describe — but the canonical synthesis machinery (TYR, TYRP1, DCT) is overwhelmingly melanocyte-specific (Human Protein Atlas Tau score 0.98/1.0 across 44 tissues and hundreds of cell types). Every non-cutaneous population discovered so far — cardiac, cochlear, leptomeningeal — was found because a disease clue forced someone to look, not from any systematic survey. The developmental logic (Schwann cell precursor seeding via peripheral nerves) predicts more undiscovered populations wherever nerves innervated organs during embryogenesis.

**Synthesis is only half the story.** Melanin can also reach cells by uptake — and this route is completely invisible to enzyme-based screening. Keratinocytes prove the concept: they contain substantial melanin, express zero TYR, and acquire it entirely via phagocytosis and EV-mediated transfer. Macrophages phagocytose extracellular melanin released from dying melanocytes and carry it through lymphatics to distant lymph nodes — systemic trafficking is real. In tumors, macrophages, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells accumulate melanoma-secreted melanosomes and have their function reprogrammed by them. Any cell with endocytic machinery can take up melanin nanoparticles in vitro. What is missing is a systematic survey asking whether this uptake occurs in healthy non-skin tissue at physiologically meaningful concentrations.

**The melanosomal vs. non-melanosomal distinction matters** for both distribution and detectability. Melanosomes are discrete, TEM-visible, ~500 nm organelles with a protein scaffold — easy to find. Non-melanosomal melanin — neuromelanin in autophagic granules, or melanin taken up into endocytic compartments after phagocytosis — is amorphous polymer with no surrounding protein scaffold. It is always compartmentalized (never loose in the cytosol, which would be toxic), but the compartment is not a canonical melanosome. This form evades melanosome-based detection: HMB-45 and anti-MART-1 IHC target melanosome proteins, not the pigment itself. It is not degraded in lysosomes and accumulates silently in the cells that take it up. The only methods that catch both forms are Fontana-Masson stain, direct anti-melanin antibodies, PAI, or TEM — none of which have been applied systematically across a full tissue panel.

The search is **thorough for active melanin synthesis in known melanocytes** but **structurally blind to four things**: (1) neuromelanin formed non-enzymatically; (2) melanin stored in post-synthetic or post-differentiation cells; (3) melanin acquired by uptake into endocytic compartments rather than synthesized; (4) non-melanosomal compartmentalized melanin vs. intact canonical melanosomes — because no systematic Fontana-Masson or anti-melanin screen across a full tissue panel has ever been published. The current map reflects what was looked for, not the full picture.

## TIER 1 — Well-Established

**Epidermal melanocytes** — primary producers. ~1 per 36 keratinocytes at the basal layer. Synthesize eumelanin and pheomelanin in melanosomes; export via dendrites.

**Keratinocytes** — recipients, not producers. Melanosomes transferred from melanocyte dendrites form a supranuclear cap over the nucleus, shielding DNA from UV. Melanosomes degrade as keratinocytes differentiate upward and desquamate.

**Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE)** — heavily melanin-loaded monolayer behind photoreceptors. Absorbs scattered light, antioxidant reservoir. With age, RPE melanin declines and lipofuscin accumulates — potentially inverting the protective role. Closely implicated in AMD. [Peters & Schraermeyer, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11745103/)

**Iris and choroidal melanocytes** — uveal tract melanocytes contain high eumelanin loads. Iris melanin density determines eye color.

**Ciliary body pigment epithelium** — melanin-loaded, forms part of the blood-aqueous barrier, modulates light reaching the lens.

**Hair follicle melanocytes** — at the bulb; insert melanin into growing keratinocytes of the hair shaft.

**Substantia nigra pars compacta (dopaminergic neurons)** — accumulate **neuromelanin** from auto-oxidation of excess cytosolic dopamine not captured by synaptic vesicles. Not in melanosomes but in autophagic, lysosome-related granules. Iron-binding provides neuroprotection; release upon neuronal death drives neuroinflammation — the Parkinson's paradox. [Fornstedt & Zetterström, PNAS 2000](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.97.22.11869) · [NM granule proteome, 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-022-02530-4) · [Neurotoxicity, Apoptosis 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10495-025-02156-3)

**Locus coeruleus (noradrenergic neurons)** — neuromelanin derived from norepinephrine; visibly blue-black in gross dissection. NM accumulates from childhood through adulthood then declines. LC neuromelanin loss closely tracks Alzheimer's progression. Notably, LC NM levels predict attentional stability and arousal regulation even in healthy young adults — a cognitive trait variable, not just a disease marker. [LC lifespan study, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614878/) · [LC neuromelanin and arousal](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.7280/D1HQ3B) · [LC in neurodegeneration](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10523397/)

**Cochlear stria vascularis melanocytes** — functionally essential intermediate cell layer of the cochlear lateral wall. Contribute to the endocochlear potential, maintain the blood-labyrinth barrier, protect against noise-induced ROS. Higher cochlear melanin correlates with lower presbycusis rates; African American individuals show higher cochlear melanin. [Human cochlear distribution, PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4521893/) · [Blood-labyrinth barrier, Shi et al., PNAS 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1205210109) · [Noise protection](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11423222/)

**Leptomeningeal melanocytes** — populate the pia mater and leptomeninges, concentrated at the ventral brainstem and upper cervical cord. MITF-dependent, melanosome-containing bona-fide melanocytes. Primary meningeal melanocytoma/melanomatosis arises from this population with no UV causation. [Brito et al., Front Neuroanat 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroanatomy/articles/10.3389/fnana.2015.00149/full) · [Human leptomeningeal atlas, Nature Comms 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42825-y)

**Melanophages (dermal macrophages)** — phagocytes, not producers. Engulf melanin granules after epidermal inflammation or tattoo deposition.

**Broader brainstem catecholaminergic neurons** — neuromelanin present at lower levels across the medulla, pons, and mesencephalon beyond SN/LC. Used as a natural histochemical marker to atlas the full catecholaminergic system. [Olson et al., J Comp Neurol 1982](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cne.901970106)

## TIER 2 — Published but Less Recognized

**Cardiac melanocytes** — neural crest-derived melanocyte-like cells expressing DCT (dopachrome tautomerase) are present in the heart and pulmonary veins in the exact regions where atrial fibrillation most commonly initiates. These cells are electrically excitable and express adrenergic and muscarinic receptors. Mice lacking *Dct* are highly susceptible to atrial arrhythmias; mice lacking both melanocyte-like cells and *Dct* fail to develop AF. *Key researchers: Levin, Patel, Epstein (U Penn).* [Levin et al., JCI 2009](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/39109) · [ROS/remodeling follow-up, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26400986/)

**Thymic epithelium** — thymic epithelial cells ectopically express melanocyte-lineage antigens (tyrosinase, MART-1) for central tolerance induction. This tolerance is so robust it limits anti-melanoma immunity. Whether true melanocytes physically reside in thymic parenchyma is anatomically unresolved. [GILT study, J Immunol 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336541/) · [Immune response limited by thymic selection, PLOS ONE 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0035005)

**Adrenal medulla (chromaffin cells)** — produce catecholamines via the same pathway as SN neurons; low-level neuromelanin formation is chemically expected. High catecholamine turnover and shorter cell lifespan likely limit accumulation. Not yet systematically studied.

**Gut / colon — DEBUNKED:** Despite the name, pseudomelanosis coli is NOT melanin. The pigment is lipofuscin — iron sulfide, silicates, and lipid peroxidation products in lamina propria macrophages. Definitively ruled out by electron microscopy. [AJG 2020](https://journals.lww.com/ajg/fulltext/2020/10001/s3172_pseudomelanosis_coli__a_historical_review.3170.aspx)

## TIER 3 — Fringe / Speculative

**Herrera — essentially ALL cells (perinuclear):** Argues melanin is present in all cell types, typically perinuclear, as a universal energy transducer via the "Solís-Herrera cycle" (2H₂O ↔ 2H₂ + O₂ + 4e⁻). Claims: melanin can be taken up into any cell's cytosol *(partially supported experimentally; not established physiologically)*; albino rat ocular tissues contain abundant melanin *(not replicated)*; perinuclear melanin is universal in eukaryotes *(not accepted)*.

**Kruse — quantum semiconductor model:** Melanin as a biological semiconductor wherever light penetrates, an active quantum energy interface linking the electromagnetic environment to mitochondrial coherence. The underlying biophysics has a real basis — eumelanin is a confirmed hybrid electronic-protonic conductor with redox potential in the mid-physiological range. The unsupported step is the scale substituting for ATP/glucose bioenergetics in vivo. [Melanin electrochemistry, RSC 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/ma/d3ma01161e) · [Melanin electronics review, 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/article/abs/pii/S0956566318307176) · [Melanin in solar cells, RSC 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2025/ma/d5ma00081e)

**"Melanin ubiquity" hypothesis:** Melanin-like polymers or precursors (5,6-dihydroxyindole oligomers) may exist at trace levels in many cell types as side-products of tyrosine and catecholamine metabolism. Chemically plausible; not demonstrated in vivo at significant levels.

## Uptake vs. Synthesis: Can Cells Acquire Melanin Without Making It?

Multiple uptake routes exist, and they have direct implications for where melanin can end up in cells that express no TYR whatsoever.

### Established Uptake Routes

**1. Keratinocyte uptake of melanosomes (canonical)**
Keratinocytes acquire melanin entirely by uptake from melanocytes — they synthesize none themselves. Two distinct mechanisms operate:
- **Melanocore exo/phagocytosis** — melanocyte releases melanosome cores; keratinocyte internalizes them via PAR-2-dependent, actin-driven phagocytosis involving Rac1, Cdc42, RhoA. [Moreiras et al., Traffic 2022](https://run.unl.pt/bitstream/10362/140396/1/Traffic_2022_Moreiras_Melanocore_uptake_by_keratinocytes_occurs_through_phagocytosis_and_involves_protease_activated.pdf)
- **Macropinocytosis** of intact melanosomes

Critically: once inside keratinocytes, melanin **resides in non-degradative, non-acidic endocytic compartments** — it avoids lysosomal degradation and **persists long-term**. This is not a transient state. [Melanin resides in nondegradative compartments, JID 2017](https://www.jidonline.org/article/S0022-202X(17)33065-8/fulltext)

**2. Extracellular vesicle (EV)-mediated transfer**
UV irradiation triggers Ca²⁺-dependent lysosomal exocytosis in melanocytes → shedding of melanin-containing EVs → preferential uptake by keratinocytes. Keratinocyte-derived EVs carrying Rac1 reciprocally promote melanocyte dendricity and melanosome maturation, amplifying the transfer loop. [PNAS 2024](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2321323121) · [Nature Sci Reports 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep27890)

**3. Macrophage phagocytosis (melanophages)**
When melanocytes die or melanin is released into the dermis, dermal macrophages phagocytose it. These melanophages carry melanin into lymphatics → regional lymph nodes. This is the established **systemic clearance pathway** for melanin — meaning melanin routinely passes through lymph nodes and potentially beyond. [Melanin clearance, Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-981-95-0233-2_5.pdf)

**4. Tumor-associated macrophage uptake (pathological)**
Melanoma-secreted melanosomes are actively taken up by tumor-associated macrophages, reprogramming their M1/M2 polarization. Secreted melanin from melanoma cells also enters dermal fibroblasts (transforming them into cancer-associated fibroblasts) and promotes angiogenesis — meaning endothelial cells and fibroblasts in a tumor microenvironment are exposed to and take up melanin. [EMBO Reports 2024](https://www.embopress.org/doi/full/10.1038/s44318-024-00103-7) · [Frontiers Oncology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fonc.2022.887366/pdf)

**5. Any cell type in vitro**
Melanin nanoparticles are taken up by essentially any cell type via standard endocytosis — hepatocytes, fibroblasts, macrophages, cancer cell lines. This is routinely exploited in drug delivery research. There is no known cell-type specificity to the uptake machinery — the question is whether extracellular melanin particles exist in non-skin tissues at concentrations sufficient for meaningful uptake.

---

### What This Means for the Distribution Question

The synthesis-enzyme atlas (TYR Tau 0.98) tells us almost no cell type is *making* melanin. But uptake is a completely independent route that the atlas is blind to. The relevant variables for uptake are:

1. **Is there extracellular melanin available?** — In healthy skin: yes, continuously. In dermis and lymphatics: yes, via melanophage trafficking. In other organs under normal conditions: probably not at significant levels. In inflammation, UV damage, or melanoma: yes, potentially systemically.

2. **Does the cell have endocytic/phagocytic machinery?** — All nucleated cells have some endocytic capacity; macrophages and dendritic cells are highly phagocytic. The PAR-2-dependent keratinocyte mechanism may be keratinocyte-specific, but non-specific endocytosis of nanoparticle-sized melanin fragments is broadly available.

3. **Is the melanin degraded or preserved?** — The keratinocyte data is striking: melanin in non-melanocytes is stored in **non-degradative compartments** and persists. If this holds in other cell types, uptake events — even rare ones — could result in permanent accumulation.

---

### Implication for Herrera's Claim

Herrera claimed melanin can be taken up from extracellular medium into any cell. This is **experimentally supported at the cell biology level** — it happens in keratinocytes (physiologically), macrophages (physiologically), and any cell type tested with melanin nanoparticles (in vitro). What remains unsupported is the claim that this uptake is physiologically significant at scale in normal non-skin tissues, and that the accumulated melanin drives energy metabolism. The uptake itself: real. The functional interpretation: not established.

## The Developmental Origin Thread

Cardiac, cochlear, and leptomeningeal melanocytes all share the same developmental origin — **Schwann cell precursors (SCPs) associated with peripheral nerves** during embryogenesis detach and differentiate into resident melanocytes in these internal sites. This is separate from the cutaneous neural crest stream. It explains why Waardenburg syndrome (EdnrB/MITF mutations) simultaneously causes skin depigmentation, deafness, and cardiac abnormalities — and predicts that melanocytes should appear wherever peripheral nerves seeded internal organs during development. Full internal map not yet drawn. [Adameyko & Ernfors, Cell Mol Life Sci 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8316242/)

## Neuromelanin as Clinical Biomarker (NM-MRI)

Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI is a fast-moving clinical field. LC NM-MRI achieves ~90% diagnostic specificity in progressive Parkinson's. SN loss tracks motor dysfunction; LC loss tracks cognitive impairment. NM-MRI differentiates PD from Alzheimer's (SN spared in AD). LC NM also predicts attentional performance in healthy adults. [BMC Neurology 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12883-023-03350-z) · [7T NM-MRI, npj Parkinson's 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00631-3) · [PD vs AD, Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2026.1672578/full) · [LC+SN, Neurology 2021](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000012444)

## How Exhaustive Is the Search? Evidence from Systematic Atlases

Two systematic approaches have actually been applied to this question — and they give a mostly reassuring but not fully satisfying answer.

### Human Protein Atlas — IHC across 44 tissues + single-cell RNA

The Human Protein Atlas has antibody-stained TYR (tyrosinase) protein across 44 normal human tissue types and mapped mRNA across hundreds of single cell types.

**Result for TYR:**
- Tau specificity score: **0.98** out of 1.0 — where 1.0 means expression in exactly one tissue and 0 means identical expression everywhere. 0.98 is about as tissue-specific as any gene gets.
- Tissue distribution category: **"Detected in single"**
- Protein expression annotation: *"Selective cytoplasmic expression in melanocytes in skin and hair, and pigment epithelium in retina"*
- Single-cell RNA: highest in melanocytes at **1,259.5 nCPM**; brain expression at **0.1 nTPM** — essentially background noise
- [TYR tissue expression, HPA](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000077498-TYR/tissue) · [TYR single cell, HPA](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000077498-TYR/single+cell)

**TYRP1 and DCT** show the same pattern — overwhelmingly melanocyte-restricted at the mRNA and protein level. One exception: **DCT clusters with Schwann cells** in HPA single-cell data (cluster "Schwann cells & Melanocytes — Mixed function") — directly consistent with the Adameyko finding that Schwann cell precursors are the developmental source of extracutaneous melanocytes.

### Single-Cell RNA Atlases (Tabula Sapiens, Allen Brain Atlas)

Melanin synthesis genes queried across hundreds of cell types in multi-organ human atlases show the same conclusion: essentially zero expression outside the melanocyte lineage. [Tabula Sapiens, Science 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abl4896)

---

### What These Surveys Tell Us — and What They Don't

**What they tell us:**
The *machinery* for melanin synthesis (TYR, TYRP1, DCT) is not being actively expressed in hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, endothelial cells, neurons, fibroblasts, or any of the hundreds of non-melanocyte cell types screened. If cells were actively making melanin via the canonical tyrosinase pathway, the atlas data would show it. It doesn't.

**What they don't tell us:**

1. **Neuromelanin is invisible to these surveys.** Neuromelanin in SN/LC neurons is made non-enzymatically — it does not require TYR expression. A cell accumulating neuromelanin-like pigment from catecholamine auto-oxidation would have no TYR/TYRP1/DCT signal and would be missed entirely by the atlas approach.

2. **Accumulated melanin without active synthesis.** A cell that synthesized melanin historically, stopped, and now retains residual polymer would show no TYR mRNA. The atlas reads current transcription, not stored pigment.

3. **Anti-melanin polymer IHC across tissues has not been done.** The atlas surveys check enzyme expression, not the pigment itself. A systematic anti-melanin antibody screen across a tissue panel — the most direct way to find stored melanin regardless of synthetic activity — has not been published.

4. **The DCT-Schwann cell signal is a prompt.** DCT expression in Schwann cells in the atlas mirrors exactly what Adameyko's lineage tracing found anatomically. It suggests the atlas data, when looked at carefully, contains signals pointing to undiscovered populations — but these require targeted follow-up, not automated interpretation.

### Verdict on Exhaustiveness

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Is TYR-dependent melanin synthesis restricted to melanocyte lineage? | **Yes — well-established by HPA + atlases** |
| Have all tissues been checked for melanocyte-lineage cells (IHC)? | **No — only targeted surveys triggered by disease clues** |
| Has anyone looked for melanin polymer directly across all tissues? | **No systematic anti-melanin IHC survey published** |
| Has neuromelanin been mapped beyond SN/LC in humans? | **Partially — brainstem atlas (1982); no modern whole-body survey** |
| Is non-enzymatic melanin (catecholamine-derived) mapped in peripheral tissues? | **No** |
| Could trace melanin exist in cells without TYR expression? | **Yes — via catecholamine auto-oxidation; undetected, not ruled out** |

**One-sentence answer:** The synthesis-enzyme surveys are thorough and reassuring for canonical melanogenesis — but they are structurally blind to neuromelanin-like pigments formed without TYR, to residual stored melanin in quiescent cells, and to any melanin that exists without active transcription. The map is not exhaustive; it is thorough within the limits of what was measured.

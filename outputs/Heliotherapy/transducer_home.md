# Melanin as Electrophysiological Transducer

---

Melanin's unique physical properties — broadband photon absorption, mixed ionic-electronic conductivity, and a proton-active surface that responds to illumination — suggest a novel anti-cancer intervention that exploits UV and visible light to shift the electrical state of melanin-containing tumor cells. Cancer cells are characteristically depolarized (Vmem ≈ −10 to −30 mV vs. −50 to −90 mV in normal cells) with alkaline intracellular pH, both of which are permissive for proliferation. Melanin, housed in melanosomes, sits at the interface between incoming photons and cell electrophysiology: photon absorption alters the polymer's charge and proton state, which perturbs the melanosome's active proton transport, which in turn propagates into cytoplasmic pH and membrane potential. Targeted light delivery to melanin-containing tumor tissue could drive both variables in the anti-proliferative direction — without a photosensitizer drug, without cytotoxic ROS, and without targeting any specific oncogene.

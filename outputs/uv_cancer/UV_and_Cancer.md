# Ultraviolet Light and Cancer: Mechanisms, Evidence, and Treatment-Relevant Claims

Humans evolved under daily solar input; some sunlight-linked signals are physiologically required or tightly coupled to health (for example, ultraviolet B–driven vitamin D synthesis and ultraviolet-linked skin photochemistry), so the right question is not “is ultraviolet good or bad,” but which spectra, doses, and patterns push which endpoints.

Ultraviolet is already a treatment modality in bounded clinical contexts (for example, medically supervised phototherapy for early-stage cutaneous T-cell lymphoma), which establishes that controlled ultraviolet exposure can shift malignant tissue behavior under specific geometry and dosing constraints.

The treatment-relevant uncertainty is how far those effects generalize: whether ultraviolet A / visible-weighted pathways, melanin photophysics, and downstream systemic mediators can be harnessed to improve cancer outcomes beyond skin-limited disease, without importing the carcinogenic mechanisms that dominate under other spectra and dose patterns.

## Systems Model

Ultraviolet input  
spectrum × dose rate × cumulative dose × intermittency × geometry  
↓  
Primary skin photochemistry  
DNA lesions and repair kinetics · oxidative chemistry · melanin photochemistry  
↓  
Local and systemic mediators  
immune tone · endocrine metabolites (vitamin D axis) · other skin-derived signals  
↓  
Cancer endpoints  
skin incidence (cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, basal cell carcinoma, melanoma) · internal incidence · progression and mortality  
↓  
Observed associations in humans  
measurement error · screening and surveillance · confounding · reverse causation

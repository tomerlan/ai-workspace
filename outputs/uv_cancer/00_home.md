# UV & Cancer — Project Home

## What this project is

Technical documentation on the relationship between **ultraviolet radiation (UV)** and **cancer**, covering:
- **Harm** (photocarcinogenesis; skin cancers as the anchor case)
- **Pleiotropy** (UV may trigger multiple pathways with mixed effects)
- **Claims of benefit** (epidemiologic inverse associations; mechanistic hypotheses; outcome-support/“curative” anecdotes)

It is **mainstream-first** (starts from the strongest consensus evidence) while also evaluating contrarian hypotheses **by topic/claim**, not by author.

## What this project is not

- Not an author-by-author critique series
- Not medical advice
- Not a “sun is good” or “sun is bad” manifesto

## Evidence-quality tags (used inline everywhere)

- **Evidence: VeryHigh**: convergent, independent lines; robust; minimal plausible bias explains away result
- **Evidence: High**: strong human evidence and/or strong mechanism; some bounded limitations remain
- **Evidence: Moderate**: suggestive but meaningful confounding/indirectness remains
- **Evidence: Low**: sparse/heterogeneous; high risk of bias; plausible but poorly constrained
- **Evidence: VeryLow**: anecdotes, ecological correlations, uncontrolled reports; hypothesis-generating only

Rule: anecdotes are allowed in the **Evidence** page, but they remain **VeryLow** unless upgraded by better data.

## What’s relatively settled (working consensus anchors)

- UV is a well-established cause of **skin cancer** (subtype- and exposure-pattern-dependent).
- UV causes **DNA damage** and also **immune and redox changes**; mechanism matters by wavelength (UVA vs UVB).
- **Melanin** is a major photoprotective system, but melanin chemistry is context-dependent (e.g., eumelanin vs pheomelanin).
- Vitamin D is biologically plausible in cancer biology, but translation to **human endpoints** depends on design (observational vs RCT vs MR).

## What’s genuinely open (the project’s “frontier questions”)

- Which reported **inverse UV–cancer associations** (e.g., for some internal cancers) survive careful control for confounding, measurement error, and surveillance bias?
- Is any UV-associated benefit mediated mostly by **vitamin D**, or by **non–vitamin D** pathways (e.g., NO/immune effects), or by “healthy outdoor” confounding?
- Do any UV/phototherapy-like exposures support **cancer outcomes** (progression/mortality) in a reproducible way, beyond anecdotes?
- Which melanin-related mechanisms are purely protective vs potentially mutagenic (e.g., chemiexcitation hypotheses) in vivo?

## Graph conventions (used throughout)

- **Arrows** indicate hypothesized causal influence, not necessarily proven causation.
- Nodes labeled with **(Evidence: …)** reflect the evidence strength for that edge/pathway.
- The **Methods** page explains confounding pathways that can mimic causal effects.

## Update policy

This project is meant to be updated. A claim can move up/down in evidence tier when:
- a higher-quality study contradicts prior work
- multiple independent lines converge (or fail to replicate)
- a key confounder/measurement bias is resolved

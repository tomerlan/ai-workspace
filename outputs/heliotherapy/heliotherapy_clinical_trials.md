# Clinical Trials: UV / Vitamin D / Light and Cancer

Indexed trials on the UV → vitamin D → melanin → cancer axis, retrieved March 2026. Organized by mechanistic category.

---

## 1. Vitamin D as Systemic Cancer Modulator

### VITAL — Vitamin D and Omega-3 Trial
**NCT01169259** | Phase III | N=25,871 | Status: Active (post-intervention follow-up)

The largest RCT on vitamin D and cancer to date. 25,871 US men and women randomized to vitamin D3 (2,000 IU/day) or omega-3 fatty acids (1g fish oil/day) vs. placebo. Primary endpoints: incident cancer and cardiovascular disease. 5-year pill-taking phase ended; long-term follow-up ongoing.

**Published results (2019–2022):** Vitamin D did *not* reduce overall cancer incidence. However, it did reduce **cancer mortality** by ~25% in a subgroup who took it ≥2 years. This is the critical distinction the VITAL investigators themselves emphasize: D may not prevent tumors from forming but may alter tumor biology toward less lethal disease. The 2,000 IU/day dose is well below the 60–100 ng/mL serum target used by the Riordan Clinic or Coimbra Protocol — making the dose-adequacy question the live debate.

None of these trials are testing the UV → melanin pathway directly; they are testing the downstream pharmacological endpoint.

### Vitamin D + Head and Neck Cancer
**[NCT05817617](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT05817617)** | Observational | Status: **Recruiting** (2023–ongoing)

Examining vitamin D levels at diagnosis and through treatment in head and neck cancer patients; tracking variation across treatment cycles. Low vitamin D is consistently associated with worse HNC prognosis in epidemiological data — this study adds longitudinal mechanistic detail.

---

### Vitamin D to Reduce Chemotherapy Side Effects (Breast Cancer)
**[NCT06642441](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT06642441)** | Randomized | Status: **Recruiting**

Evaluating whether vitamin D supplementation reduces adverse effects of adjuvant chemotherapy in breast cancer. A supportive-care framing that may nonetheless provide survival signal data.

---

### Vitamin D + Immune Reactivity in Oral Cancer
**[NCT00953849](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00953849)** | Phase I/II | N=21 | Status: Completed

Combined vitamin D + celecoxib to stimulate intratumoral immune reactivity in oral cancer. Two-pronged approach: vitamin D as immune modulator + COX-2 inhibition to reduce immune suppression. A rare trial directly targeting the immune-stimulation axis rather than pure differentiation or anti-proliferation. Primary outcomes: changes in intratumoral IL-2, IFN-γ, GM-CSF, and IL-6 — all markers of T-cell activation and anti-tumor immune function.

## 2. Extracorporeal Photopheresis (ECP) — UV-Mediated Immune Modulation

ECP is the closest thing on the registry to clinical UV blood irradiation. Mechanism: patient blood is passed extracorporeally through a system that treats leukocytes with 8-methoxypsoralen (8-MOP) + UVA light, then reinfuses them. The treated cells trigger immune reprogramming — reducing autoreactive T-cells and restoring immune surveillance.

| NCT | Indication | Phase | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NCT03563040](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT03563040) | Mycosis fungoides / Sézary syndrome (cutaneous T-cell lymphoma) | II | Withdrawn (not safety-related) |
| [NCT00057954](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00057954) | Lymphoma — reduced-intensity conditioning before allogeneic BMT | II | Terminated |
| [NCT00179790](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00179790) | Pediatric leukemia/cancer — ECP as BMT conditioning | I/II | Completed |
| [NCT00930566](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00930566) | Hematological malignancies — ECP pilot | I/II | Unknown |
| [NCT00045305](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00045305) | Myelodysplastic syndromes — ECP + pentostatin + TBI before BMT | II | Completed |
| [NCT00005092](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00005092) | Leukemia/lymphoma/myeloma — psoralen-treated donor cells | I | Completed |

**Take:** ECP is not UV as a systemic modulator in the heliotherapy sense — it is UV as a targeted ex vivo leukocyte treatment. However, it demonstrates that UV-induced immune modulation is mechanistically real, clinically reproducible, and sufficiently robust to enter transplant conditioning protocols. Mycosis fungoides / Sézary syndrome is the clearest application: ECP is used as a frontline immunotherapy for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma and is FDA-cleared for GVHD.

## 3. UVB as Immune Modifier in Cancer Treatment

### UVB + Allogeneic Stem Cell Transplantation for Hematologic Malignancies
**[NCT00068523](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00068523)** | Status: Completed

Explored UVB light therapy administered before and after allogeneic stem cell transplantation in leukemia, lymphoma, myeloma, and myelodysplastic syndromes. Rationale: UVB modulates T-cell activity, potentially reducing graft rejection while preserving graft-versus-tumor effect.

---

### UVB-Irradiated Tumor Cell Vaccine (Vax-DC/MM) — Multiple Myeloma
**[NCT02248402](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT02248402)** | Phase I/II | Status: Unknown (enrollment opened 2013)

Mechanistically the most directly relevant trial to the UV/melanin/immune hypothesis. Autologous myeloma cells are irradiated with UVB to induce immunogenic cell death, then loaded onto patient-derived dendritic cells. The resulting vaccine (Vax-DC/MM) is reinfused to activate anti-tumor immunity. The UVB step is not incidental — it converts otherwise immune-tolerant tumor cells into immunogenic antigens.

---

### UVB vs. Supplementation — Immune Function Comparison
**[NCT03609970](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT03609970)** | Status: Completed

Directly compared immune outcomes from UVB exposure (sun-derived vitamin D) vs. oral vitamin D3 supplementation. Key question: do the additional UVR-induced mediators (beyond vitamin D) provide independent immune benefits? Several studies show inverse correlations between UV exposure and immune-mediated diseases unexplained by vitamin D alone. This trial is one of the only registered attempts to isolate the contribution of non-vitamin-D UV photoproducts.

---

### UVB LED Device for Vitamin D Synthesis + Cancer Risk Markers
**[NCT06625918](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT06625918)** | Status: Completed (2024)

Tested daily UVB LED device exposure to raise vitamin D levels in adults, assessing both serum 25(OH)D response and markers of skin inflammation. Listed conditions include "Skin Melanoma." Completed September 2024 — data not yet published as of March 2026.

## 4. UV-Induced Immunosuppression — Mechanism Studies

### RANKL Inhibition to Block UV-Induced Immunosuppression
**[NCT01978483](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01978483)** | Phase I | Status: Completed

Tested whether denosumab (anti-RANKL antibody) could block the UV-induced immunosuppression that allows skin cancers to evade immune clearance. RANKL signaling is the mechanistic link: UV activates keratinocyte RANKL, which inactivates skin-resident dendritic cells and T-cells. Denosumab blocking this pathway restored normal contact hypersensitivity after UVB. Implication: UV-induced immune suppression is pharmacologically reversible — and the RANKL axis is a potential target for preventing UV-associated skin cancer beyond sunscreen.

## 5. UV Damage as Predictive Biomarker in Cancer Treatment

### Dermatoheliosis as Predictor of Anti-PD-1 Response in Melanoma
**[NCT05856565](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT05856565)** | Status: **Recruiting** (2023–2028)

An AI-based algorithm is being developed to quantify perilesional dermatoheliosis — visible UV-accumulated skin damage around a melanoma's excision scar — as a surrogate for tumor mutational burden (TMB). The logic: melanomas arising from chronic UV mutagenesis carry high TMB; high TMB predicts response to anti-PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy. Dermatoheliosis is a free, non-invasive, naked-eye visible proxy that could guide treatment selection without expensive genomic sequencing.

**Relevance to this framework:** This trial inverts the standard UV narrative. UV history is not purely risk — in melanoma that has already formed, a high-UV-damage background predicts better immunotherapy response and likely better survival. The UV mutagenesis that created the tumor also created an immune-visible target. Whether this extends to a broader protective role of UV in tumor immunosurveillance is an open question the trial does not address — but the mechanistic logic points in that direction.

## Summary: What the Trial Landscape Shows

| Axis | Status on ClinicalTrials.gov |
|---|---|
| UV → Vitamin D → cancer prevention (population level) | Extensively trialed (VITAL, D-HEALTH); vitamin D reduces cancer *mortality* but not clear incidence at standard doses |
| Calcitriol (active D) as cancer sensitizer/differentiator | ~10 completed Phase I/II trials; prostate cancer focus; clinical signal present, no Phase III |
| UV ex vivo (ECP) → immune modulation for blood/lymph cancers | FDA-cleared for CTCL/GVHD; multiple completed Phase I/II trials |
| UVB irradiation of tumor cells as immunogenic vaccine | One Phase I/II trial (myeloma, Vax-DC/MM); status unclear |
| UV immunosuppression reversal (RANKL blockade) | One completed Phase I; mechanism confirmed |
| UV damage as immunotherapy predictor (dermatoheliosis) | One active recruiting trial (2023–2028); innovative framing |
| Systemic sunlight/heliotherapy as primary cancer intervention | **Zero trials registered.** The historical sanatorium approach has no modern RCT equivalent. |

The last row is the critical gap. The entire heliotherapy tradition — graduated full-body sun as primary systemic treatment — exists in a pre-RCT evidence state. Everything on the registry represents downstream pharmacological arms (calcitriol) or ex vivo light manipulation (ECP), not the full biological cascade that heliotherapy engaged: UV → melanin photoexcitation → systemic electron transfer → mitochondrial/immune effects.

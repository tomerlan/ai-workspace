# Photomelanin: A Primer
---

## Motivation

Melanin is a broadband photon-absorbing polymer embedded in skin and distributed — less recognized — across cardiac tissue, the cochlea, leptomeninges, and the retinal pigment epithelium. Sunlight governs its biological state. Melanin may be relevant to oncology beyond its role in skin protection against UV.

Two interventions follow. The first rebuilds the skin's adaptive photoprotective state through graduated UV exposure. The second targets the tumor's electrical phenotype by driving photon-induced electrochemical shifts in melanin-loaded cells. Neither requires a novel drug. Both require treating light as a primary biological variable rather than a hazard to minimize.

---

## First Principles

### Evolutionary Prior

Humans evolved under daily solar input. Biology had to become compatible with sunlight as a background condition, and sunlight-linked signals became embedded in physiology as a result. The skin's melanin system, the UV-driven vitamin D axis, nitric oxide mobilization, photoimmune calibration — these are not incidental; they are biological programs that require light to operate correctly.

Compatibility is conditional. Regular, non-burning exposures drive adaptation and mediator pathways. Intermittent high-dose peaks that cause burns load the DNA-damage side without building the adaptive infrastructure. Modern indoor living followed by recreational overexposure is the worst of both configurations: adaptation channels atrophied, damage peaks intact.

### UV as a Vector

"UV exposure" is not a single variable. Biology responds to a vector: **spectrum × intermittency × geometry × host modifiers**. These determine which channels dominate.

UVB (280–315 nm) drives direct DNA photoproduct formation — cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers and 6-4 photoproducts — and is the primary driver of vitamin D3 synthesis. UVA (315–400 nm) penetrates deeper, acts predominantly through photosensitizers and redox chemistry, and is the band relevant to melanin coupling, NO mobilization, and immune modulation. Standard UV metrics are erythema-weighted toward UVB; a low UV index day carries substantial UVA dose.

### Signal vs. Damage

The same photons can act as signals on short timescales and as damage on long timescales. UVA/visible-weighted exposure at appropriate doses drives redox shifts, mediator release, and melanin-linked charge and proton dynamics — control variables that move cellular set points. UVB-weighted or high-dose exposure accumulates DNA lesions that translate into mutational burden over years. These channels can compete; the practical consequence is that spectrum and dose pattern determine outcome, not UV exposure per se.

This is the basis for both interventions. Prevention targets adaptation through the signal channel. Treatment targets tumor electrical state through UVA-melanin coupling — the same signal channel, used deliberately under controlled conditions.

## Melanin: Structure, Distribution, and Measurement

### Structure and Chemistry

Melanin is a high-molecular-weight aromatic polymer built from oxidized DHI and DHICA indole units, crosslinked irregularly into a three-dimensional network. Its structural disorder is the functional feature: the heterogeneous conjugated system produces broadband, featureless absorption from UV through near-infrared. Sharp molecular transitions would produce photochemical reactivity; disorder produces flat, efficient energy dissipation across all wavelengths.

Three biologically distinct forms exist with different synthetic routes, photochemistries, and clinical relevance.

**Eumelanin** (brown-black) is the UV-protective form. It absorbs broadly, dissipates energy as heat, and contains stable semiquinone radicals detectable by EPR at g ≈ 2.004. It is synthesized via the Raper-Mason pathway in melanosomes and transferred to keratinocytes.

**Pheomelanin** (yellow-red) diverges from eumelanin at the dopaquinone branch point when cysteine is available. Under UVA it generates superoxide and singlet oxygen rather than dissipating energy. MC1R loss-of-function shifts the eu:pheo balance toward pheomelanin. The eu:pheo ratio — not total melanin content — is the operative quality variable. Mice with constitutively high pheomelanin develop melanoma in complete darkness (Mitra et al., Nature 2012), establishing pheomelanin as an independent mutagenic system.

**Neuromelanin** is produced by non-enzymatic auto-oxidation of excess cytosolic dopamine and norepinephrine in substantia nigra and locus coeruleus neurons. It requires no TYR, forms in no melanosome, and has no clearance mechanism. It accumulates from birth, loads iron progressively (Fe³⁺ at ~1 per 5 DHI equivalents), and protects neurons while they survive — driving neuroinflammation when they die.

The master regulator of enzymatic melanin synthesis is **MITF**, which simultaneously drives TYR, TYRP1, DCT, and melanosome structural protein expression. Melanin polymerizes inside the **melanosome**, a lysosome-related organelle maturing through four stages from fibrillar scaffold to fully opaque polymer mass. Mature Stage IV melanosomes are transferred from melanocyte dendrites to keratinocytes, where they form **supranuclear caps** — a polarized UV shield on the sun-facing side of each cell's nucleus. Photoprotection depends on cap geometry as much as melanin quantity.

### Tissue Distribution

Canonical melanin distribution understates the actual map. Cochlear stria vascularis melanocytes are functionally required for the endocochlear potential and potassium recycling; their absence explains melanin-correlated differences in noise-induced hearing loss across populations. Cardiac neural-crest-derived melanocytes expressing DCT occupy the pulmonary vein sleeves and interatrial myocardium — exactly the sites where atrial fibrillation most commonly initiates. Mice lacking DCT-positive cardiac cells show dramatically elevated arrhythmia rates (Levin et al., JCI 2009). Leptomeningeal melanocytes populate the ventral brainstem and cervical cord; primary meningeal melanocytoma arises from this population with no UV involvement.

All three extracutaneous populations share a developmental origin: Schwann cell precursors that detached from peripheral nerves during embryogenesis and differentiated as resident melanocytes. Waardenburg syndrome (MITF/EDNRB mutations) simultaneously ablates all of them — skin depigmentation, deafness, and cardiac dysfunction from a single genetic lesion.

Synthesis is not the only route to melanin accumulation. Keratinocytes express no TYR and acquire melanin entirely by phagocytosis of melanocyte-released melanosome cores and by EV-mediated transfer. Internalized melanin resides in non-degradative endocytic compartments and persists long-term. In melanoma, secreted melanosomes reprogram tumor-associated macrophages and transform dermal fibroblasts into cancer-associated fibroblasts.

### Detection and Quantification

Fontana-Masson silver stain — the clinical standard — achieves 56% overall sensitivity across tissue types and fails for neuromelanin. Its false-negative rate in amelanotic melanoma is clinically significant.

The relevant panel for exhaustive tissue surveys is IHC with SOX10 + MART-1 + DCT. These markers detect melanocyte-lineage cells regardless of melanin content, including quiescent and amelanotic populations — the cells missed by all pigment-based methods. This is the panel that identified cardiac, cochlear, and leptomeningeal melanocytes.

HPLC-AHPO degrades melanin to marker compounds (eumelanin → PTCA; pheomelanin → TTCA) and quantifies both forms absolutely from bulk tissue. This defines eu:pheo ratio — the operative clinical variable.

For in vivo imaging: photoacoustic microscopy detects single melanocytes at sub-micron resolution with 4 mm depth penetration. Multiphoton FLIM resolves melanin by its sub-0.2 ns fluorescence lifetime, distinct from all other skin fluorophores, enabling 3D quantitative mapping through the full epidermis. Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI at 3T detects iron-loaded NM in SN and LC with ~90% diagnostic specificity for Parkinson's disease.

The structural blind spot: melanin taken up into non-melanosomal endocytic compartments carries no melanosome protein scaffold and is readily confused with lipofuscin or hemosiderin on TEM. No systematic anti-melanin antibody survey across a full tissue panel has been published. The current distribution map reflects what was looked for.

## UV and Cancer: The Actual Evidence Structure

The policy claim — avoid all UV — was calibrated on SCC and BCC data and incorrectly extended to melanoma and internal cancers.

**SCC and BCC.** The causal chain is settled. UV-signature mutations (C→T and CC→TT at dipyrimidines, COSMIC SBS7a/7b) dominate SCC and BCC genomes, appear in pre-cancerous actinic keratoses, and are found at high frequency in normal-appearing sun-exposed skin (Martincorena et al., Science 2015). Xeroderma pigmentosum — inherited NER deficiency — produces 1,000-fold elevated UV-skin cancers with complete tissue specificity.

**Melanoma.** The dose-pattern distinction is epidemiologically supported and mechanistically explained. Intermittent high-dose exposure and burns elevate risk; chronic occupational UV is neutral to inversely associated across multiple meta-analyses (Gandini et al., 2005). The MC1R/pheomelanin confound is largely uncorrected in the literature: fair-skin MC1R-null phenotypes simultaneously carry more pheomelanin (UV-independent mutagenic system), less eumelanin, and lower NER capacity. Attributing risk differences between pigmentation groups to UV dose conflates three distinct variables.

A further complication: the majority of UV-signature CPDs in melanocytes form after UV exposure ends. NOS and NOX remain activated post-UV, producing peroxynitrite that reacts with melanin synthesis intermediates to generate electronically excited carbonyls driving CPD formation without photons — the chemiexcitation mechanism (Premi et al., Science 2015). Post-exposure oxidative conditions, not UV dose alone, determine mutagenic output in melanocytes.

**Internal cancers.** Latitude-inverse associations exist for colon, breast, prostate, and lymphoma in ecological analyses. Individual-level causal evidence is weak. The VITAL trial (N=25,871, vitamin D3 2,000 IU/day, ~5 years) found no significant reduction in cancer incidence — establishing that supplementation does not replicate the population-level signal. Sunlight drives co-pathways — UVA-mediated NO mobilization, skin immune calibration, circadian entrainment — that oral supplementation cannot replicate.

Photoimmunology data further supports this structure: UV is an immune-state regulator, not only a mutagenic input. Immune effects are dose-pattern dependent and can be either harmful (surveillance suppression in burn/high-dose patterns) or adaptive (calibrated signaling under constrained non-burning patterns), so protocol and monitoring determine direction.

## Heliotherapy: Clinical Precedent

Deliberate, graduated sunlight as primary treatment preceded the antibiotic era and produced documented outcomes that were never systematically revisited after pharmacotherapy displaced the practice.

**Niels Finsen** received the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for demonstrating that concentrated UV cured lupus vulgaris in controlled clinical series. He founded the first dedicated phototherapy clinic in Copenhagen in 1896.

**Auguste Rollier** (Leysin, Switzerland, 1903–1954) operated 36 clinics at over 1,000 beds. His protocol was graduated — beginning with brief distal exposure, progressing proximally over weeks, erythema treated as protocol failure. His post-WWI records: 1,746 recovered of 2,167 patients treated. *Heliotherapy* (1923) documents the methodology. The practice was abandoned when sulfonamides and penicillin arrived, not because outcomes failed. No controlled follow-up has been conducted since.

The same historical record is also evidence of non-skin reach. Heliotherapy reports from that era include not only cutaneous TB responses, but also bone and pulmonary TB cohorts treated in sanatoria. By modern standards these datasets are heterogeneous and mostly uncontrolled, but they still establish a key translational point: structured light protocols can alter systemic disease trajectories beyond skin-local effects.

## Intervention 1: The Solar Callus

Repeated suberythemal UV exposure produces a coordinated adaptive state — increased eumelanin content, improved cap geometry, thicker stratum corneum, upregulated NER, and better post-UV oxidative handling — that is mechanistically distinct from the light-naive skin of modern indoor-living patients.

Five adaptive processes are co-induced:

**Melanin induction and redistribution.** UV → keratinocyte p53 → POMC → α-MSH → MC1R → cAMP → MITF → TYR expression → eumelanin synthesis. The same cAMP signal shifts eu:pheo toward eumelanin independently of net synthesis rate. Mature melanosomes transfer to keratinocytes and organize into supranuclear caps.

**Epidermal hyperplasia.** Repeated suberythemal UV thickens the stratum corneum. Minimum erythema dose increases 1.4–2.3× in published human trials (Farr et al. 1999; Felton et al. 2020).

**NER upregulation.** MC1R/cAMP/CREB signaling upregulates nucleotide excision repair capacity in melanocytes independently of pigmentation. Melanin synthesis and repair capacity are co-induced, not competing outcomes.

**Post-UV oxidative conditioning.** The chemiexcitation window — peroxynitrite driving dark CPD formation 2–4 hours post-UV — is modulated by the post-exposure redox environment. Omega-3:omega-6 ratio and glutathione availability directly affect dark CPD burden per UV event.

**Photoimmune calibration.** Graduated exposure induces regulatory T-cell populations in draining lymph nodes and calibrates Langerhans cell function. The distinction from immunosuppression is dose and pattern, not wavelength.

**Protocol:** Begin at sub-erythema dose by Fitzpatrick type and real-time UV index. Advance 3–5 minutes per session, gated by absence of erythema in the prior 24 hours. Gate to higher-UV sessions at MED ≥1.4× baseline. Primary functional benchmark: MED ≥2× baseline. Mechanistic quality marker: eu:pheo ratio by HPLC-AHPO.

Cancer patients present in the worst possible callus state — chronically indoors, vitamin D depleted, maximally light-naive. Standard oncological guidance (avoid UV, supplement D) addresses none of the above. The callus also functions as the prerequisite substrate for Intervention 2: the electrophysiological mechanism requires loaded, mature melanosomes, and that requires the callus to be built first.

## Melanin as Electrophysiological Transducer: A Working Hypothesis

Melanin in hydrated form is a mixed ionic-electronic conductor. Electronic conduction runs through the conjugated aromatic polymer; protonic conduction runs along the quinone/hydroquinone groups distributed throughout the backbone — analogous to proton-coupled electron transfer in the mitochondrial ETC. Hydration increases conductivity by orders of magnitude. Illumination measurably increases electronic conductivity via photoinduced charge separation (Mostert et al., PNAS 2012; Bothma et al., PNAS 2008). Melanin is also a photoacid: UV absorption shifts the effective pKa of surface quinone groups, causing transient proton release from the polymer surface into the surrounding medium.

The melanosome is an active electrochemical organelle. Its membrane maintains an acidic lumen (pH ~4.5–5.5) via a V-ATPase. OCA2 regulates lumenal pH as its primary function — loss-of-function causes oculocutaneous albinism type 2 by abolishing the acidic environment required for melanogenesis. The polymer inside is not a passive absorber; it is a photoacidic conductor in intimate contact with pH-sensitive transporters embedded in the same membrane.

The proposed chain: photon absorption → proton release at polymer surface → transient modulation of OCA2 and V-ATPase activity within the Debye length (~1 nm) at the lumenal membrane face → collective melanosome proton exchange → shift in cytoplasmic pHi microdomains → altered conductance of pHi-sensitive inward-rectifier K⁺ channels → plasma membrane hyperpolarization → reduced voltage-gated Ca²⁺ entry → mitochondrial membrane potential shift.

Cancer cells are characteristically depolarized (Vmem ≈ −10 to −30 mV vs. −50 to −90 mV in differentiated cells) and maintain alkaline intracellular pH (~7.4–7.6 vs. ~7.0–7.2). Both are causally upstream of proliferative programs. The Levin lab demonstrated in Xenopus that depolarizing normal melanocytes to cancer-compatible Vmem induces melanoma-like growth without genetic mutation, and that restoring Vmem arrests it. Blocking the G1→S transition requires preventing the pHi alkalinization that is mechanistically required for S-phase entry. If the proposed coupling chain holds, suberythemal UV delivered to melanin-loaded cells shifts both variables simultaneously in the anti-proliferative direction — acting on the physical state variable rather than a specific oncogene.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Melanin is a mixed ionic-electronic conductor | Established |
| Melanin is photoconductive | Established |
| Melanosome maintains acidic lumen via active ion transport | Established |
| Vmem depolarization causally induces cancer-like phenotype | Established (Levin lab) |
| UV drives proton release from melanin surface in intact melanosomes | Not measured |
| Proton release couples to cytoplasmic pHi and Vmem shift | Not demonstrated in cells |
| Suberythemal UVA shifts Vmem in a melanin-dependent manner | Not demonstrated |

Every component is individually grounded. The chain as a unit has not been demonstrated.

## Intervention 2: Light-Driven Electrophysiology

Light delivered to melanin-loaded tumor cells at sub-cytotoxic doses, targeting Vmem and pHi rather than inducing oxidative cell death. The intended effect is cytostatic — a state change in proliferative phenotype. This is mechanistically distinct from photodynamic therapy, which delivers photosensitizer-amplified oxidative bursts to ablate tissue.

**Wavelength by depth:**

| Lesion depth | Wavelength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Surface (epidermis/dermis) | UVA 315–400 nm | Peak melanin absorption |
| 1–3 mm | 405–450 nm | Shallow tissue window |
| Deeper lesion | 630–680 nm | ~1 cm tissue penetration |

Melanin confirmation in the target tissue is a prerequisite — T1-MRI for internal lesions, dermoscopy or biopsy for surface lesions. Amelanotic melanoma is excluded.

Response assessment: FDG-PET (glycolytic rate — earliest sensitivity to metabolic state change), Ki-67 biopsy, RECIST imaging.

A sequential preclinical gating program is required before clinical use. Experiment 1: lumenal pH shift in isolated Stage III–IV melanosomes under UVA, absent in TYR-null controls. Experiment 2: cytoplasmic pHi shift in intact melanocytes, absent in amelanotic controls. Experiment 3: Vmem response by voltage-sensitive dye and patch clamp — direction and melanin-dependence. Experiment 4: action spectrum — response must track melanin absorption, not be spectrally flat (which would indicate a thermal artifact). Experiment 5: functional cancer readout — reduced proliferation in pigmented versus amelanotic melanoma cells, cytostatic not cytotoxic. Experiments 1–3 are the minimum gating set; if all succeed, the core mechanism is established and the clinical dose can be specified.

## Practitioners and Centers

No modern clinic practices heliotherapy in the Rollier sense. The closest active equivalents are integrative oncology programs that treat UV and vitamin D as systematic biological inputs.

**Riordan Clinic** (Wichita, KS) — non-profit, operating since 1975, FABNO-credentialed oncology practitioners. Targets 25(OH)D at 60–100 ng/mL; IV vitamin C as backbone therapy. The most credible integrative oncology program in North America by conventional markers.

**BioCare Hospital** (Tijuana) — 40+ years continuous operation. UV blood irradiation as immune modulator; conservative integrative positioning alongside conventional care.

**Michael Holick, MD PhD** (Boston University) — most cited researcher on sunlight dosing and vitamin D metabolism, with skin-type and latitude-specific UV protocols. Documented conflicts with tanning-industry and supplement funders; critics concede the underlying biology is sound.

**Cedric Garland, DrPH** (UC San Diego) — developed the DINOMIT model of vitamin D and cancer prevention; primary author of the systematic dose-response epidemiological literature.

## Conclusion

Oncology lacks leverage on the physical and systemic variables that set the cellular context in which tumors grow. Membrane potential and intracellular pH are not downstream readouts — they are upstream regulators of proliferative programs. Eumelanin content and eu:pheo ratio are not cosmetic variables — they determine mutagenic load per UV event and photoprotective capacity of the tissue.

The solar callus is a recoverable state. It was the default under pre-industrial light exposure; it is measurably superior at managing UV-driven mutagenesis compared with the light-naive skin of modern patients. Building it back is intervention with a century of indirect support and no pharmaceutical equivalent.

The electrophysiological mechanism is a hypothesis grounded in established physics and cell biology that has not yet been demonstrated at the cellular level. Experiments 1–3 of the preclinical program will establish or refute it. If they succeed, Intervention 2 represents a mechanistically distinct approach — not a cytotoxin, not a kinase inhibitor, but a photonic modulator of the bioelectric state that gates cancer's proliferative program.

# Title
- Ventricular enlargement in mental illness attributed to CSF–melanin–light “human photosynthesis” via a proposed water dissociation/reformation cycle

# Detailed Reconstruction
Neuroimaging and earlier pneumoencephalographic work are used to motivate that ventricular enlargement (ventriculomegaly) and increased subarachnoid space are repeatedly observed in schizophrenia and related disorders, often alongside evidence interpreted as reduced brain volume (e.g., enlarged sulci) and selected regional volume reductions (e.g., hippocampus). Ventricular abnormality is treated as a robust structural correlate, present at illness onset and often nonprogressive, with additional reported associations such as male predominance and correlation with hospitalization frequency. Standard explanatory frames are characterized as emphasizing neurodevelopmental delay and/or cortical atrophy, with CSF treated as a passive filler.

A different mechanistic interpretation is then built around a proposed bioenergetic premise: visible and invisible light is asserted to be the true energy source for the CNS, with melanin as the principal transducer and water as the substrate. CSF—being mostly water—is positioned as a dynamic energy substrate rather than a static fluid. The anatomical arrangement of mammalian CNS surfaces and cavities bathed in CSF (ventricles and subarachnoid space) is interpreted as an evolved design to keep water in constant contact with neurons, enabling continuous energy generation and distribution.

A specific bidirectional reaction (“Solis-Herrera cycle”) is proposed to occur continuously in melanin:  
2H₂O (l) ↔ 2H₂ (g) + O₂ (g) + 4e⁻.  
The dissociation direction is described as energetically costly in conventional laboratory conditions but asserted to be driven rapidly in vivo by melanin harvesting energy from the electromagnetic spectrum. Molecular hydrogen is treated as the primary energy carrier produced by water splitting, while oxygen is characterized as toxic at any concentration. The reverse reaction (water reformation) is described as energetically “cheaper” and as producing both re-formed water and an “orderly flow” of high-energy electrons. Energy distribution is conceptualized as diffusion-like “growing spheres” of alternating hydrogen gas and re-formed water permeating intracellular space, supplying activation energy broadly to biochemical reactions.

Ventricular size is then linked to the balance of dissociation versus reformation: ventricular volume is asserted to be determined by the water dissociation/re-association ratio. Reduced dissociation is proposed to increase water abundance, enlarging ventricles and impairing cellular energy availability in a spatially nonuniform way, producing functional disturbances and, with sufficient duration, tissue changes that could be interpreted as cortical atrophy. Environmental pollutants are proposed to damage melanin, shrinking the effective “sphere” of energy distribution and promoting tissue deterioration. Exogenous oxygen therapy is proposed to worsen the balance by accelerating water reformation, consuming hydrogen, lowering tissue energy, and producing edema/ventricular enlargement.

The conclusion generalizes the bioenergetic thesis beyond schizophrenia: glucose is framed as biomass (carbon skeleton) rather than an energy source, with thermodynamic arguments asserted to preclude glucose providing energy for its own metabolism. Neurological alterations associated with hypo- or hyperglycemia are attributed to disturbances in a mass–energy balance rather than to glucose-derived energy failure. Brain sulci are reinterpreted as structures ensuring neuronal proximity to CSF water as the energy source. Neuromelanin-rich nuclei (e.g., locus coeruleus) are proposed to perform the same light-to-chemical-energy transduction; loss of locus coeruleus neurons in Alzheimer’s disease is interpreted as diminished neuromelanin-derived chemical energy leading to neuronal dysfunction. A buoyancy-like claim is added: high-metabolism zones are expected above pigmented areas because hydrogen rises.

# Explicit Claims (>= 20; numbered)

1. Claim: Ventricular enlargement was reported decades ago as associated with a clinical diagnosis of schizoaffective illness.  
   - Alignment: Supported

2. Claim: Mental disorders show changes in cerebral ventricle volume beyond daily physiological variation.  
   - Alignment: Supported

3. Claim: Research attention has more often focused on nervous tissue alterations than on ventricular volumetric changes.  
   - Alignment: Partially supported

4. Claim: Melanin can transform light energy into chemical energy through dissociation of water, analogous to chlorophyll in plants.  
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: No accepted biochemical pathway shows melanin catalyzing photolysis of water to H₂, O₂, and electrons in humans; known human bioenergetics relies on redox metabolism (e.g., mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation), not melanin-driven water splitting.

5. Claim: The real source of CNS energy is visible and invisible light.  
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: CNS energy is overwhelmingly supplied by oxidation of metabolic substrates (primarily glucose; also lactate/ketones) delivered via blood flow; light does not penetrate to power deep brain metabolism at meaningful levels.

6. Claim: Melanin is the principal energy transducer in the CNS, with water as its “perfect substrate.”  
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Neuromelanin is not recognized as an energy-transducing system; its mainstream roles relate to pigment biology, metal binding, and potential involvement in neurodegeneration, not primary ATP generation.

7. Claim: Mammalian CNS has an invariant pattern of being coated inside and out by CSF that is 99% water to keep water constantly in contact with neurons.  
   - Alignment: Partially supported

8. Claim: Brain sulci (“caesuras”) exist to allow water to reach the most recondite places of the brain.  
   - Alignment: Speculative

9. Claim: Ventricles and subarachnoid space are the source of CNS energy.  
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Ventricles/subarachnoid space contain CSF for cushioning, waste clearance, and homeostasis; energy for neurons is generated intracellularly from metabolic substrates, not produced by CSF spaces.

10. Claim: Changes in ventricular/subarachnoid volumetrics must be interpreted as significant alterations in generation and distribution of chemical energy.  
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Ventricular size changes are interpreted in relation to neurodevelopment, atrophy, hydrocephalus/CSF dynamics, and tissue loss; they are not established markers of altered chemical energy generation.

37. Claim: Molecular oxygen is toxic at any concentration and at any level.

# Technical Constructs

- Term: Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)  
  - Definition: Clear extracellular fluid produced mainly by choroid plexus, circulating through ventricles and subarachnoid space; supports buoyancy, cushioning, solute transport, and waste clearance.  
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Ventriculomegaly / ventricular enlargement  
  - Definition: Increased volume of cerebral ventricles relative to expected norms, often reflecting altered neurodevelopment, tissue loss, or CSF dynamics.  
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Subarachnoid space enlargement  
  - Definition: Increased CSF space between arachnoid and pia mater, often accompanying cortical volume loss or altered CSF dynamics.  
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Pneumoencephalography  
  - Definition: Historical imaging technique replacing CSF with air to visualize ventricular system/brain contours on radiographs.  
  - Mainstream status: Established (historical/obsolete)

- Term: CT / MRI volumetry  
  - Definition: Imaging-based quantification of brain structures and CSF spaces to assess regional and global volume differences.  
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Dopamine hypothesis (schizophrenia)  
  - Definition: Framework implicating dysregulated dopaminergic signaling (especially striatal hyperdopaminergia) in psychosis, with broader circuit refinements.  
  - Mainstream status: Established (with refinements)

- Term: Neuromelanin  
  - Definition: Dark polymeric pigment in catecholaminergic neurons (e.g., substantia nigra, locus coeruleus), associated with catecholamine metabolism and metal binding.  
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: “Human Photosynthesis®”  
  - Definition: Proposed process in which melanin converts light into chemical energy by splitting water in human tissues.  
  - Mainstream status: Fringe

- Term: “Solis-Herrera cycle”  
  - Definition: Proposed bidirectional reaction 2H₂O ↔ 2H₂ + O₂ + 4e⁻ occurring in melanin to generate chemical energy and regulate CSF volume.  
  - Mainstream status: Fringe

- Term: Molecular hydrogen (H₂) as biological energy carrier  
  - Definition: Proposed primary carrier of energy released from melanin-driven water dissociation, distributing usable energy through tissues.  
  - Mainstream status: Fringe

- Term: Oxygen toxicity “at any concentration”  
  - Definition: Claim that O₂ is universally toxic regardless of dose.  
  - Mainstream status: Fringe

# Logical Flow

2. Interpretation -> Ventricular enlargement is treated as a robust structural correlate; conventional explanations emphasize neurodevelopmental delay and/or cortical atrophy with passive CSF filling.  
3. New premise -> Light (visible/invisible) is asserted to be the true CNS energy source; melanin is asserted to transduce light into chemical energy using water.  
4. Mechanism -> A bidirectional melanin-mediated water dissociation/reformation reaction (2H₂O ↔ 2H₂ + O₂ + 4e⁻) is proposed to run continuously, producing hydrogen-carried energy and electron flow.  
5. Link to anatomy -> CSF (mostly water) bathing ventricles/subarachnoid space is positioned as the energy substrate kept near neurons by brain morphology.  
6. Causal bridge -> Ventricular volume is asserted to depend on the dissociation/reformation balance; reduced dissociation increases water, enlarging ventricles and causing uneven energy distribution.  
7. Pathophysiology -> Energy impairment is proposed to drive cognitive/clinical manifestations and, with duration, tissue changes labeled as cortical atrophy.  
8. Modifiers -> Pollutants are proposed to damage melanin and shrink energy distribution; exogenous oxygen is proposed to worsen hydrogen availability and promote edema/ventricular enlargement.  
9. Broader implication -> Glucose is reframed as biomass-only; water splitting is framed as the dominant energy source across eukaryotic cells, requiring reorganization of biology/medicine.

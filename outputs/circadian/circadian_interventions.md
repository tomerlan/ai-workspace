# Interventions
## Chapter 2 — The Intervention Catalog

---

This chapter catalogs interventions that can stabilize or optimize circadian organization. Chapter 1 (Measurements) defines how to assess circadian health. This chapter defines **what** can be done. Chapter 3 (Protocol) will define **when** — the conditional logic that maps measurement deviations to intervention selection.

Each entry describes the intervention, its rationale, and the circadian dimensions it affects. The protocol uses this catalog as its action set.

---

## 1. Zeitgeber Hierarchy

Not all inputs are equal. Circadian control is hierarchical:

- **1. Light** — Sets SCN phase; overrides most downstream signals
- **2. SCN outputs** — Melatonin, temperature, cortisol; coordinate periphery
- **3. Feeding** — Strong entrainer of liver, gut, pancreas; can oppose SCN if misaligned
- **4. Activity** — Moderate entrainer via temperature and metabolism
- **5. Social / behavioral** — Weak; supportive but not authoritative

**Override rules:** Light overrides melatonin. Feeding overrides liver clock. Chronic conflict between cues produces desynchrony rather than adaptation.

---

## 2. Light

### Morning outdoor light

**What:** Outdoor light exposure shortly after waking. Eyes exposed to ambient daylight. Artificial bright light if outdoor exposure unavailable.

**Rationale:** Strongest phase-advancing cue. Increases circadian amplitude and consolidates sleep. Melanopsin-mediated SCN entrainment is intensity- and spectrum-dependent; natural daylight provides appropriate spectral composition.

**Affects:** Phase (advance), amplitude, sleep consolidation.

---

### Evening melanopic suppression

**What:** Begin dimming lights well before sleep. Avoid blue-rich and overhead lighting. Minimize close-range screen use late in the evening.

**Rationale:** Melanopic (blue) light suppresses melatonin and delays phase. Evening exposure extends biological day and compresses biological night.

**Affects:** Phase (prevents delay), melatonin amplitude, sleep onset.

---

### True darkness during sleep

**What:** Sleeping environment maintained near complete darkness. Cover LEDs, eliminate ambient light. Brief low-intensity red light only if necessary for safety.

**Rationale:** Maintains nocturnal melatonin amplitude and hormonal separation of night and day. Even low-level light during sleep can suppress melatonin and fragment rhythms.

**Affects:** Melatonin amplitude, endocrine day–night contrast, repair-phase integrity.

---

### Bright days (amplitude reinforcement)

**What:** Accumulate substantial daytime brightness, including the morning session. Prioritize outdoor exposure when possible.

**Rationale:** Circadian robustness depends on contrast between bright days and dark nights. Low daytime light flattens amplitude even with good night darkness.

**Affects:** Amplitude, rest–activity rhythmicity, metabolic day–night contrast.

---

### Morning red / near-infrared exposure

**What:** Morning exposure to red or near-infrared light via sunlight or standardized device.

**Rationale (Kruse-inspired):** Hypothesized mitochondrial redox and nitric-oxide signaling effects. Morning red/infrared may precondition mitochondria before higher-energy exposures.

**Affects:** Mitochondrial priming, redox state.

---

### Solar-sequence conditioning

**What:** Morning outdoor light; optional controlled midday sun exposure appropriate to skin type; strict dark nights.

**Rationale (Kruse-inspired):** Hormetic model. Proper sequencing — red/infrared priming (morning), UV/adaptive stress (midday), darkness (night) — may enhance circadian amplitude.

**Affects:** Amplitude, repair capacity, UV tolerance.

---

## 3. Sleep

### Wake-time anchor

**What:** Fixed wake time every day. Bedtime flexible but sufficient for adequate sleep.

**Rationale:** Wake time most strongly anchors central circadian phase and reduces phase noise. Consistency of rise time stabilizes the entire system.

**Affects:** Phase stability, DLMO reproducibility, sleep midpoint consistency.

---

### Night cooling

**What:** Maintain a cooler sleep environment within comfort limits. Optional daytime warmth exposure.

**Rationale:** Temperature rhythms support sleep consolidation and circadian signaling. Core temperature decline facilitates sleep onset; night cooling reinforces the day–night thermal contrast.

**Affects:** Sleep consolidation, temperature rhythm amplitude, autonomic–circadian coupling.

---

## 4. Feeding

### Daytime-aligned feeding

**What:** All caloric intake confined to daytime window. Consistent first and last meal timing. Avoid late-night eating.

**Rationale:** Feeding strongly entrains peripheral metabolic clocks (liver, gut, pancreas). Night feeding creates metabolic states permissive to proliferation and desynchronizes periphery from SCN.

**Affects:** Peripheral clock alignment, metabolic rhythms, glucose stability, internal desynchrony.

---

## 5. Activity

### Exercise earlier in the active phase

**What:** Schedule exercise in the morning or mid-afternoon. Avoid vigorous activity late in the evening.

**Rationale:** Exercise acts as a secondary circadian cue via temperature and metabolism. Late timing elevates arousal, raises core temperature, and delays sleep.

**Affects:** Phase, peripheral clock coordination, sleep onset, temperature rhythm.

---

### Morning priming and evening downshifting

**What:** Combine early light exposure with light movement in the morning. Reduce cognitive and sensory load in the evening.

**Rationale (Zaid-inspired):** Hypothesized reduction of circadian noise and improved segregation of biological day and night. Evening cognitive/sensory load may extend biological day.

**Affects:** Phase coherence, amplitude, day–night segregation.

---

## 6. Consistency

### Conflicting zeitgeber elimination

**What:** Align light, sleep, feeding, and activity schedules consistently across weekdays and weekends. Minimize day-to-day drift.

**Rationale:** Misaligned cues fragment internal circadian coordination. Social jetlag (weekend sleep shift) is a common source of desynchrony.

**Affects:** Phase stability, cross-system coherence, DLMO reproducibility.

---

### Social schedule stabilization

**What:** Minimize late nights and irregular social timing.

**Rationale:** Social jetlag introduces phase instability. Variable bedtimes and wake times obscure true circadian phase.

**Affects:** Phase stability, measurement interpretability.

---

## 7. Masking and Modifiers

### Caffeine control

**What:** Fixed early-day cutoff. No caffeine in the afternoon or evening.

**Rationale:** Caffeine masks sleep pressure and can distort actigraphy, temperature, and subjective sleep metrics.

**Affects:** Measurement validity; indirectly supports circadian alignment by preserving sleep pressure signal.

---

### Alcohol avoidance

**What:** Avoid alcohol during assessment and optimization phases.

**Rationale:** Alcohol disrupts sleep architecture (suppresses REM, fragments sleep) and circadian hormone rhythms. Masks true circadian state.

**Affects:** Measurement validity; sleep architecture; melatonin and cortisol rhythms.

---

### Electromagnetic exposure minimization

**What:** Remove phones from the bedroom or use airplane mode. Avoid charging devices near bed. Optional wired networking at night.

**Rationale (Kruse-inspired):** Hypothesized effects of non-native EMF on calcium handling, redox state, and circadian stability.

**Affects:** Redox, calcium signaling, circadian stability.

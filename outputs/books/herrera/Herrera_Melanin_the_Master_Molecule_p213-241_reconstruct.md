# Title
- Age-related macular degeneration and Alzheimer’s disease linked via amyloid/oxidative pathology and a proposed melanin–water “Human Photosynthesis®” energy mechanism; critique of anti-VEGF therapy and presentation of animal/clinical observations

# Detailed Reconstruction
Age is positioned as a shared dominant risk factor for Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and age-related macular degeneration (AMD), with additional shared pathological motifs: β-amyloid (Aβ) deposition, oxidative stress, and apoptotic cell death. Aβ is treated as a cross-disease hallmark because it appears in senile plaques in AD and in drusen in AMD. AMD is framed as a late-onset neurodegenerative retinal disorder causing progressive central vision loss through dysfunction and death of photoreceptors and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) at the macula. Wet (neovascular) AMD is described as less prevalent than dry (atrophic) AMD but responsible for most severe vision loss, with choroidal neovascularization and fibrous tissue as defining features. VEGF is treated as a key driver of neovascular AMD, while anti-VEGF therapy is argued to have limited durability, require repeated intravitreal injections, and carry ocular/systemic risks.

A broader vascular/angiogenesis theme is developed: angiogenesis is linked to hypoxia and inflammatory/mechanical stimuli; VEGF is described as central to endothelial survival, proliferation, migration, and permeability, and as essential for embryonic vascular development (with VEGF haploinsufficiency described as lethal). In AD, pro-angiogenic signaling is described as upregulated in brain microvessels and yet increased vascularity is said to lack evidence; instead, hypoperfusion and reduced microvascular density are emphasized. A parallel is drawn to dry AMD, which is also described as having hypoperfusion and reduced microvascular density. The ordering of causal processes leading to neuronal/photoreceptor impairment is treated as unresolved, with many risk factors listed (e.g., smoking, solar exposure, cardiovascular disease).

A central mechanistic proposal is introduced: melanin is asserted to have an intrinsic capacity to split and reform water, converting light energy into chemical energy in a process labeled “Human Photosynthesis®” or the “Solís-Herrera Cycle®,” represented as \(2H_2O \leftrightarrow 2H_2 + O_2 + 4e^-\). This is positioned as analogous to the first step of plant photosynthesis, with a further claim that plants cannot reform water whereas humans can. The proposal redefines bioenergetics: glucose/food is asserted to provide biomass but not energy; blood vessels are asserted to transport building blocks but not energy; water dissociation is asserted to be the true energy source, with diatomic hydrogen framed as the energy carrier. Sunlight is asserted to be the “universal nutrient,” and a Light/Melanin/Water system is proposed as a general photosynthetic apparatus in humans (and possibly universally).

Therapeutically, “enhancing” this proposed melanin-driven water dissociation is presented as a primary intervention that can restore downstream biochemical balance and thereby improve AMD and AD. Evidence is presented in two forms: (i) an animal model in which topical corneal exposure to a compound identified as \(C_6H_5OH\) is said to downregulate mammalian “photosynthesis” and induce corneal neovascularization in a concentration-dependent manner; (ii) multiple clinical case vignettes and images describing substantial improvements in retinal anatomy and visual acuity after administration of a “Human Photosynthesis® enhancer” (QIAPI 1™ / QIAPINE™) given sublingually at frequent dosing intervals. The narrative further argues that acute slowing of “Human Photosynthesis®” yields edema/hemorrhage, while chronic slowing yields fibrosis/mitosis.

Anti-VEGF monoclonal antibodies are criticized on mechanistic and safety grounds. Intravitreal anti-VEGF is asserted to distribute systemically and to cause systemic complications requiring hospitalization in ~25% of treated patients. A further mechanism is proposed: repeated anti-VEGF injections induce immune responses not only to the drug but also against native VEGF receptors (via “mirror image” similarity), causing endothelial dysfunction, persistent harm, and paradoxical increases in VEGF concentrations. Anti-VEGF is also linked to a shift from angiogenesis to fibrosis via CTGF upregulation, and to metastatic promotion. Vitamins/antioxidants/supplements are described as of uncertain benefit, with a cited meta-analysis asserting increased all-cause mortality from antioxidant supplements and a statement that no proven preventive treatment exists for AMD.

The conclusion generalizes the melanin–water dissociation concept to the three leading causes of blindness (AMD, diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma), arguing that pathogenesis should be reconsidered because melanin has been historically treated as a sunscreen/light absorber rather than as a primary energy transducer.

# Explicit Claims (>= 20; numbered)

1. Claim: Age is a common risk factor for both Alzheimer’s disease and age-related macular degeneration.
   - Alignment: Supported

2. Claim: Alzheimer’s disease and age-related macular degeneration share pathological hallmarks including Aβ accumulation, oxidative stress, and apoptotic cell death.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

3. Claim: Aβ is present in senile plaques in Alzheimer’s disease brain tissue.
   - Alignment: Supported

4. Claim: Aβ is present in drusen in age-related macular degeneration.
   - Alignment: Supported

5. Claim: AMD is a late-onset neurodegenerative retinal disease causing progressive central vision loss due to dysfunction/death of photoreceptors and RPE at the macula.
   - Alignment: Supported

6. Claim: Wet (neovascular) AMD is less prevalent than dry (atrophic) AMD but accounts for most severe visual loss from AMD.
   - Alignment: Supported

7. Claim: Wet AMD is characterized by choroidal angiogenesis accompanied by fibrous tissue.
   - Alignment: Supported

8. Claim: VEGF is a key contributory factor in the pathophysiology of neovascular AMD.
   - Alignment: Supported

9. Claim: Anti-VEGF therapies have “short life” and limited efficacy and do not improve wet AMD cases.
   - Alignment: Contested  
   - Contradiction note: Large randomized trials show anti-VEGF agents (e.g., ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab off-label) often improve or stabilize vision in neovascular AMD, though durability and treatment burden are limitations.

10. Claim: Repeated intravitreal injections carry serious risks to visual function.
   - Alignment: Supported

11. Claim: AMD is multifactorial and the relative contributions of genetic and environmental factors remain unknown.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

12. Claim: Advanced age is the strongest known risk factor for AMD.
   - Alignment: Supported

13. Claim: AD is multifactorial and affected by age, environmental factors, and genetics.
   - Alignment: Supported

14. Claim: AD hallmarks include senile plaques, neurofibrillary tangles, neuronal cell loss, reactive gliosis, and sometimes cerebrovascular amyloid deposits.
   - Alignment: Supported

15. Claim: Senile plaques are generated by deposition and accumulation of amyloid beta peptides.
   - Alignment: Supported

16. Claim: In AD brain, factors/processes characteristic of vascular activation and angiogenesis are found, but evidence for increased vascularity is lacking.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

17. Claim: Cerebral hypoperfusion and decreased microvascular density are major clinical features in AD.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

18. Claim: In dry AMD there is retinal and choroidal hypoperfusion and decreased microvascular density.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

19. Claim: Melanin has an intrinsic property to split and reform the water molecule.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Mainstream biophysics/biochemistry does not support melanin catalyzing bulk water-splitting into \(H_2\) and \(O_2\) under physiological conditions; no accepted pathway shows sustained in vivo hydrogen production from water via melanin.

20. Claim: Retinal tissue obtains energy from water rather than from glucose.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Retinal metabolism is highly dependent on glucose (aerobic glycolysis in photoreceptors and oxidative metabolism in RPE/inner retina); water is not an energy substrate in human cells.

21. Claim: The reaction \(2H_2O \leftrightarrow 2H_2 + O_2 + 4e^-\) occurs in humans as a physiological energy-generating process.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: This reaction is not part of known human metabolism; generating \(H_2\) and \(O_2\) from water requires substantial energy input and specialized catalysts (e.g., photosystem II in plants), not evidenced in human tissues.

22. Claim: The analogous plant reaction is \(2H_2O \rightarrow 2H_2 + O_2\).
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Oxygenic photosynthesis splits water to release \(O_2\), protons, and electrons; it does not produce molecular hydrogen \(H_2\) as the main product.

23. Claim: Plants can split water but cannot reform it, whereas humans split and reform water in a cycle.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Plants continuously form water in metabolism (e.g., respiration) and in photosynthetic electron transport; the proposed human water-splitting/reforming cycle is not established.

24. Claim: Diatomic hydrogen is the primary energy carrier “by excellence” in the universe and transports the energy released by water dissociation.
   - Alignment: Speculative

25. Claim: Food provides biomass but cannot be a source of energy.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Oxidation of dietary macronutrients (glucose, fatty acids, amino acids) is the primary source of ATP in humans; food is both energy and biomass source.

26. Claim: Blood vessels cannot transport energy and only carry building blocks (glucose, amino acids, lipids, electrolytes).
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Circulation transports chemical energy substrates (glucose, fatty acids, ketones, lactate) whose oxidation yields ATP; “energy transport” in physiology commonly refers to substrate delivery.

27. Claim: Sunlight is the real source of energy for the human eukaryotic cell.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Humans are not photoautotrophs; cellular energy is derived from chemical energy in nutrients via mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis.

28. Claim: A human “photosynthesis system” composed of Light/Melanin/Water converts photonic energy into chemical energy.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: No mainstream evidence supports a melanin-mediated photochemical water-splitting apparatus producing usable cellular energy in humans.

29. Claim: Medically intensifying the proposed melanin-driven process would improve multiple organs (joints, heart, lung, kidney, intestine, brain, eye).
   - Alignment: Speculative

30. Claim: Enhancing “Human Photosynthesis®” yields highly significant average visual function improvement of ~50–80% in AMD.
   - Alignment: Speculative

31. Claim: AD patients treated with enhancement of “Human Photosynthesis®” show regression of signs and symptoms.
   - Alignment: Speculative

32. Claim: A rat experiment using topical corneal application of \(C_6H_5OH\) produced concentration-dependent corneal neovascularization in exposed eyes, with no neovascularization in contralateral control eyes.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

33. Claim: Slowing “Human Photosynthesis®” acutely causes edema and hemorrhage; slowing it chronically causes fibrosis or mitosis in any tissue.
   - Alignment: Speculative

34. Claim: Intravitreal anti-VEGF distributes systemically to organs (liver, brain, heart, intestines, lung).
   - Alignment: Partially supported

35. Claim: About one quarter of patients receiving intraocular anti-VEGF injections have systemic complications requiring hospitalization.
   - Alignment: Contradicted  
   - Contradiction note: Large studies show systemic serious adverse event rates are much lower; while systemic exposure occurs, hospitalization in ~25% is not consistent with pharmacovigilance and trial data.

36. Claim: Anti-VEGF antibody effects are fleeting and favor growth of metastases.
   - Alignment: Contested  
   - Contradiction note: In oncology, anti-VEGF can improve outcomes in some cancers; claims of promoting metastasis are debated and context-dependent, not a general established effect.

37. Claim: Bevacizumab is commonly used in the USA for neovascular AMD despite absence of large-scale clinical-trial data supporting its use.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

38. Claim: In non-vitrectomized human eyes, aqueous half-life of 1.5 mg intravitreal bevacizumab is ~9.82 days.
   - Alignment: Supported

39. Claim: Intravitreal bevacizumab improves mfERG measures versus baseline but does not restore them to normal.
   - Alignment: Supported

40. Claim: Antioxidant supplements increase all-cause mortality.
   - Alignment: Contested  
   - Contradiction note: Some meta-analyses reported increased mortality for certain antioxidants in certain datasets, but findings vary by supplement type, dose, population, and study inclusion; not a universal consensus.

41. Claim: No treatment is known that prevents development of age-related macular degeneration.
   - Alignment: Partially supported

   - Alignment: Supported

# Technical Constructs

- Term: Age-related macular degeneration (AMD)
  - Definition: Degenerative retinal disease affecting macula, causing central vision loss; includes dry (atrophic) and wet (neovascular) forms.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Alzheimer’s disease (AD)
  - Definition: Progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline and neuropathology including Aβ plaques and tau tangles.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Drusen
  - Definition: Extracellular deposits between RPE and Bruch’s membrane; associated with AMD risk/progression.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Amyloid beta (Aβ)
  - Definition: Peptides derived from APP processing; aggregate in AD plaques; also detected in some ocular deposits including drusen.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor)
  - Definition: Signaling protein family regulating angiogenesis, vascular permeability, and endothelial survival.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Anti-VEGF therapy
  - Definition: Pharmacologic inhibition of VEGF signaling (e.g., ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab) to treat neovascular retinal disease.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Choroidal neovascularization (CNV)
  - Definition: Pathologic growth of new vessels from choroid into/under retina, central in wet AMD.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Hypoperfusion
  - Definition: Reduced blood flow to tissue, potentially contributing to hypoxia and dysfunction.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: Electroretinogram (ERG / mfERG)
  - Definition: Electrophysiologic measurement of retinal responses to visual stimuli; mfERG maps localized retinal function.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: “Human Photosynthesis®” / “Solís-Herrera Cycle®”
  - Definition: Proposed melanin-mediated photochemical splitting/reforming of water to generate chemical energy in humans.
  - Mainstream status: Fringe

- Term: Water-splitting reaction \(2H_2O \rightarrow O_2 + 4H^+ + 4e^-\)
  - Definition: Core oxygen-evolving step of oxygenic photosynthesis catalyzed by photosystem II in plants/cyanobacteria.
  - Mainstream status: Established

- Term: CTGF (connective tissue growth factor)
  - Definition: Profibrotic mediator implicated in extracellular matrix production and fibrosis; sometimes discussed in retinal scarring contexts.
  - Mainstream status: Established

# Logical Flow
1. Shared epidemiology -> Advanced age increases risk for both AD and AMD.
2. Shared pathology -> Both conditions show extracellular deposits and Aβ involvement; oxidative stress/apoptosis are implicated.
3. Vascular framing -> Hypoperfusion and altered angiogenic signaling are associated with neurodegeneration in brain and retina.
4. Therapeutic limitation -> Anti-VEGF addresses neovascularization but is argued to be transient and risky.
5. Proposed root mechanism -> Cellular energy supply is asserted to depend on melanin-driven light-to-chemical conversion via water splitting/reforming.
6. Disease causation link -> Reduced “Human Photosynthesis®” is proposed to drive edema/hemorrhage acutely and fibrosis/mitosis chronically, contributing to AMD/AD pathology.
7. Experimental support -> A chemical inhibitor (phenol) is used in rats to downregulate the process and induce neovascularization.
8. Clinical support -> Sublingual “enhancers” are associated with reported improvements in AMD (and AD) signs and function.
9. Implication -> Enhancing the proposed process could treat/prevent multiple degenerative diseases simultaneously.
